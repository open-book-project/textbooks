    Glossary¶

    The key terms of every chapter, in alphabetical order. Each entry ends with a link to the chapter where the term is defined.

    A¶

    absolute pressure
    sum of gauge pressure and atmospheric pressure (Chapter 14)
    acceleration due to gravity
    acceleration of an object as a result of gravity (Chapter 3)
    acceleration vector
    instantaneous acceleration found by taking the derivative of the velocity function with respect to time in unit vector notation (Chapter 4)
    accuracy
    the degree to which a measured value agrees with an accepted reference value for that measurement (Chapter 1)
    action-at-a-distance force
    type of force exerted without physical contact (Chapter 13)
    amplitude (A)
    maximum displacement from the equilibrium position of an object oscillating around the equilibrium position (Chapter 15)
    angular acceleration
    time rate of change of angular velocity (Chapter 10)
    angular frequency
    \(\omega,\) rate of change of an angle with which an object that is moving on a circular path (Chapter 4)
    angular momentum
    rotational analog of linear momentum, found by taking the product of moment of inertia and angular velocity (Chapter 11)
    angular position
    angle a body has rotated through in a fixed coordinate system (Chapter 10)
    angular velocity
    time rate of change of angular position (Chapter 10)
    anticommutative property
    change in the order of operation introduces the minus sign (Chapter 2)
    antinode
    location of maximum amplitude in standing waves (Chapter 16)
    antiparallel vectors
    two vectors with directions that differ by \(180{}^{\circ}\) (Chapter 2)
    aphelion
    farthest point from the Sun of an orbiting body; the corresponding term for the Moon’s farthest point from Earth is the apogee (Chapter 13)
    apparent weight
    reading of the weight of an object on a scale that does not account for acceleration (Chapter 13)
    Archimedes’ principle
    buoyant force on an object equals the weight of the fluid it displaces (Chapter 14)
    associative
    terms can be grouped in any fashion (Chapter 2)
    average acceleration
    the rate of change in velocity; the change in velocity over time (Chapter 3)
    average power
    work done in a time interval divided by the time interval (Chapter 7)
    average speed
    the total distance traveled divided by elapsed time (Chapter 3)
    average velocity
    the displacement divided by the time over which displacement occurs under constant acceleration (Chapter 3)

    B¶

    banked curve
    curve in a road that is sloping in a manner that helps a vehicle negotiate the curve (Chapter 6)
    base quantity
    physical quantity chosen by convention and practical considerations such that all other physical quantities can be expressed as algebraic combinations of them (Chapter 1)
    base unit
    standard for expressing the measurement of a base quantity within a particular system of units; defined by a particular procedure used to measure the corresponding base quantity (Chapter 1)
    beat frequency
    frequency of beats produced by sound waves that differ in frequency (Chapter 17)
    beats
    constructive and destructive interference of two or more frequencies of sound (Chapter 17)
    Bernoulli’s equation
    equation resulting from applying conservation of energy to an incompressible frictionless fluid: \(p+\frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}+\rho gh=\text{constant},\) throughout the fluid (Chapter 14)
    Bernoulli’s principle
    Bernoulli’s equation applied at constant depth: \(p_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\rho v_{1}^{2}=p_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\rho v_{2}^{2}\) (Chapter 14)
    black hole
    mass that becomes so dense, that it collapses in on itself, creating a singularity at the center surround by an event horizon (Chapter 13)
    bow wake
    v-shaped disturbance created when the wave source moves faster than the wave propagation speed (Chapter 17)
    breaking stress (ultimate stress)
    value of stress at the fracture point (Chapter 12)
    bulk modulus
    elastic modulus for the bulk stress (Chapter 12)
    bulk strain
    (or volume strain) strain under the bulk stress, given as fractional change in volume (Chapter 12)
    bulk stress
    (or volume stress) stress caused by compressive forces, in all directions (Chapter 12)
    buoyant force
    net upward force on any object in any fluid due to the pressure difference at different depths (Chapter 14)

    C¶

    center of gravity
    point where the weight vector is attached (Chapter 12)
    center of mass
    weighted average position of the mass (Chapter 9)
    centripetal acceleration
    component of acceleration of an object moving in a circle that is directed radially inward toward the center of the circle (Chapter 4)
    centripetal force
    any net force causing uniform circular motion (Chapter 6)
    closed system
    system for which the mass is constant and the net external force on the system is zero (Chapter 9)
    commutative
    operations can be performed in any order (Chapter 2)
    component form of a vector
    a vector written as the vector sum of its components in terms of unit vectors (Chapter 2)
    compressibility
    reciprocal of the bulk modulus (Chapter 12)
    compressive strain
    strain that occurs when forces are contracting an object, causing its shortening (Chapter 12)
    compressive stress
    stress caused by compressive forces, only in one direction (Chapter 12)
    conservative force
    force that does work independent of path (Chapter 8)
    conserved quantity
    one that cannot be created or destroyed, but may be transformed between different forms of itself (Chapter 8)
    constructive interference
    when two waves arrive at the same point exactly in phase; that is, the crests of the two waves are precisely aligned, as are the troughs (Chapter 16)
    conversion factor
    a ratio that expresses how many of one unit are equal to another unit (Chapter 1)
    Coriolis force
    inertial force causing the apparent deflection of moving objects when viewed in a rotating frame of reference (Chapter 6)
    corkscrew right-hand rule
    a rule used to determine the direction of the vector product (Chapter 2)
    critically damped
    condition in which the damping of an oscillator causes it to return as quickly as possible to its equilibrium position without oscillating back and forth about this position (Chapter 15)
    cross product
    the result of the vector multiplication of vectors is a vector called a cross product; also called a vector product (Chapter 2)

    D¶

    density
    mass per unit volume of a substance or object (Chapter 14)
    derived quantity
    physical quantity defined using algebraic combinations of base quantities (Chapter 1)
    derived units
    units that can be calculated using algebraic combinations of the fundamental units (Chapter 1)
    destructive interference
    when two identical waves arrive at the same point exactly out of phase; that is, precisely aligned crest to trough (Chapter 16)
    difference of two vectors
    vector sum of the first vector with the vector antiparallel to the second (Chapter 2)
    dimension
    expression of the dependence of a physical quantity on the base quantities as a product of powers of symbols representing the base quantities; in general, the dimension of a quantity has the form \(\text{L}^{\text{a}}\text{M}^{\text{b}}\text{T}^{\text{c}}\text{I}^{\text{d}}\Theta^{\text{e}}\text{N}^{\text{f}}\text{J}^{\text{g}}\) for some powers a, b, c, d, e, f, and g. (Chapter 1)
    dimensionally consistent
    equation in which every term has the same dimensions and the arguments of any mathematical functions appearing in the equation are dimensionless (Chapter 1)
    dimensionless
    quantity with a dimension of \(\text{L}^{0}\text{M}^{0}\text{T}^{0}\text{I}^{0}\Theta^{0}\text{N}^{0}\text{J}^{0}=1;\) also called quantity of dimension 1 or a pure number (Chapter 1)
    direction angle
    in a plane, an angle between the positive direction of the x-axis and the vector, measured counterclockwise from the axis to the vector (Chapter 2)
    discrepancy
    the difference between the measured value and a given standard or expected value (Chapter 1)
    displacement
    change in position (Chapter 2)
    displacement
    the change in position of an object (Chapter 3)
    displacement vector
    vector from the initial position to a final position on a trajectory of a particle (Chapter 4)
    distance traveled
    the total length of the path traveled between two positions (Chapter 3)
    distributive
    multiplication can be distributed over terms in summation (Chapter 2)
    Doppler effect
    alteration in the observed frequency of a sound due to motion of either the source or the observer (Chapter 17)
    Doppler shift
    actual change in frequency due to relative motion of source and observer (Chapter 17)
    dot product
    the result of the scalar multiplication of two vectors is a scalar called a dot product; also called a scalar product (Chapter 2)
    drag force
    force that always opposes the motion of an object in a fluid; unlike simple friction, the drag force is proportional to some function of the velocity of the object in that fluid (Chapter 6)
    dynamics
    study of how forces affect the motion of objects and systems (Chapter 5)

    E¶

    elapsed time
    the difference between the ending time and the beginning time (Chapter 3)
    elastic
    collision that conserves kinetic energy (Chapter 9)
    elastic
    object that comes back to its original size and shape when the load is no longer present (Chapter 12)
    elastic limit
    stress value beyond which material no longer behaves elastically and becomes permanently deformed (Chapter 12)
    elastic modulus
    proportionality constant in linear relation between stress and strain, in SI pascals (Chapter 12)
    elastic potential energy
    potential energy stored as a result of deformation of an elastic object, such as the stretching of a spring (Chapter 15)
    energy conservation
    total energy of an isolated system is constant (Chapter 8)
    English units
    system of measurement used in the United States; includes units of measure such as feet, gallons, and pounds (Chapter 1)
    equal vectors
    two vectors are equal if and only if all their corresponding components are equal; alternately, two parallel vectors of equal magnitudes (Chapter 2)
    equilibrium
    body is in equilibrium when its linear and angular accelerations are both zero relative to an inertial frame of reference (Chapter 12)
    equilibrium point
    position where the assumed conservative, net force on a particle, given by the slope of its potential energy curve, is zero (Chapter 8)
    equilibrium position
    position where the spring is neither stretched nor compressed (Chapter 15)
    escape velocity
    initial velocity an object needs to escape the gravitational pull of another; it is more accurately defined as the velocity of an object with zero total mechanical energy (Chapter 13)
    estimation
    using prior experience and sound physical reasoning to arrive at a rough idea of a quantity’s value; sometimes called an “order-of-magnitude approximation,” a “guesstimate,” a “back-of-the-envelope calculation”, or a “Fermi calculation” (Chapter 1)
    event horizon
    location of the Schwarzschild radius and is the location near a black hole from within which no object, even light, can escape (Chapter 13)
    exact differential
    is the total differential of a function and requires the use of partial derivatives if the function involves more than one dimension (Chapter 8)
    explosion
    single object breaks up into multiple objects; kinetic energy is not conserved in explosions (Chapter 9)
    external force
    force acting on an object or system that originates outside of the object or system (Chapter 5)
    external force
    force applied to an extended object that changes the momentum of the extended object as a whole (Chapter 9)

    F¶

    first equilibrium condition
    expresses translational equilibrium; all external forces acting on the body balance out and their vector sum is zero (Chapter 12)
    fixed boundary condition
    when the medium at a boundary is fixed in place so it cannot move (Chapter 16)
    flow rate
    abbreviated Q, it is the volume V that flows past a particular point during a time t, or \(Q=dV\text{/}dt\) (Chapter 14)
    fluids
    liquids and gases; a fluid is a state of matter that yields to shearing forces (Chapter 14)
    force
    push or pull on an object with a specific magnitude and direction; can be represented by vectors or expressed as a multiple of a standard force (Chapter 5)
    force constant (k)
    characteristic of a spring which is defined as the ratio of the force applied to the spring to the displacement caused by the force (Chapter 15)
    free boundary condition
    exists when the medium at the boundary is free to move (Chapter 16)
    free fall
    the state of movement that results from gravitational force only (Chapter 3)
    free fall
    situation in which the only force acting on an object is gravity (Chapter 5)
    free-body diagram
    sketch showing all external forces acting on an object or system; the system is represented by a single isolated point, and the forces are represented by vectors extending outward from that point (Chapter 5)
    frequency (f)
    number of events per unit of time (Chapter 15)
    friction
    force that opposes relative motion or attempts at motion between systems in contact (Chapter 6)
    fundamental
    the lowest-frequency resonance (Chapter 17)
    fundamental frequency
    lowest frequency that will produce a standing wave (Chapter 16)

    G¶

    gauge pressure
    pressure relative to atmospheric pressure (Chapter 14)
    gravitational field
    vector field that surrounds the mass creating the field; the field is represented by field lines, in which the direction of the field is tangent to the lines, and the magnitude (or field strength) is inversely proportional to the spacing of the lines; other masses respond to this field (Chapter 13)
    gravitational torque
    torque on the body caused by its weight; it occurs when the center of gravity of the body is not located on the axis of rotation (Chapter 12)
    gravitationally bound
    two object are gravitationally bound if their orbits are closed; gravitationally bound systems have a negative total mechanical energy (Chapter 13)

    H¶

    harmonics
    the term used to refer collectively to the fundamental and its overtones (Chapter 17)
    hearing
    perception of sound (Chapter 17)
    Hooke’s law
    in a spring, a restoring force proportional to and in the opposite direction of the imposed displacement (Chapter 5)
    hydraulic jack
    simple machine that uses cylinders of different diameters to distribute force (Chapter 14)
    hydrostatic equilibrium
    state at which water is not flowing, or is static (Chapter 14)

    I¶

    ideal banking
    sloping of a curve in a road, where the angle of the slope allows the vehicle to negotiate the curve at a certain speed without the aid of friction between the tires and the road; the net external force on the vehicle equals the horizontal centripetal force in the absence of friction (Chapter 6)
    ideal fluid
    fluid with negligible viscosity (Chapter 14)
    impulse
    effect of applying a force on a system for a time interval; this time interval is usually small, but does not have to be (Chapter 9)
    impulse-momentum theorem
    change of momentum of a system is equal to the impulse applied to the system (Chapter 9)
    inelastic
    collision that does not conserve kinetic energy (Chapter 9)
    inertia
    ability of an object to resist changes in its motion (Chapter 5)
    inertial force
    force that has no physical origin (Chapter 6)
    inertial reference frame
    reference frame moving at constant velocity relative to an inertial frame is also inertial; a reference frame accelerating relative to an inertial frame is not inertial (Chapter 5)
    instantaneous acceleration
    acceleration at a specific point in time (Chapter 3)
    instantaneous angular acceleration
    derivative of angular velocity with respect to time (Chapter 10)
    instantaneous angular velocity
    derivative of angular position with respect to time (Chapter 10)
    instantaneous speed
    the absolute value of the instantaneous velocity (Chapter 3)
    instantaneous velocity
    the velocity at a specific instant or time point (Chapter 3)
    intensity (I)
    power per unit area (Chapter 16)
    interference
    overlap of two or more waves at the same point and time (Chapter 16)
    internal force
    force that the simple particles that make up an extended object exert on each other. Internal forces can be attractive or repulsive (Chapter 9)

    K¶

    Kepler’s first law
    law stating that every planet moves along an ellipse, with the Sun located at a focus of the ellipse (Chapter 13)
    Kepler’s second law
    law stating that a planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times, meaning it has a constant areal velocity (Chapter 13)
    Kepler’s third law
    law stating that the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of the orbit (Chapter 13)
    kilogram
    SI unit for mass, abbreviated kg (Chapter 1)
    kinematics
    the description of motion through properties such as position, time, velocity, and acceleration (Chapter 3)
    kinematics of rotational motion
    describes the relationships among rotation angle, angular velocity, angular acceleration, and time (Chapter 10)
    kinetic energy
    energy of motion, one-half an object’s mass times the square of its speed (Chapter 7)
    kinetic friction
    force that opposes the motion of two systems that are in contact and moving relative to each other (Chapter 6)

    L¶

    laminar flow
    type of fluid flow in which layers do not mix (Chapter 14)
    law
    description, using concise language or a mathematical formula, of a generalized pattern in nature supported by scientific evidence and repeated experiments (Chapter 1)
    law of conservation of angular momentum
    angular momentum is conserved, that is, the initial angular momentum is equal to the final angular momentum when no external torque is applied to the system (Chapter 11)
    Law of Conservation of Momentum
    total momentum of a closed system cannot change (Chapter 9)
    law of inertia
    see Newton’s first law of motion (Chapter 5)
    lever arm
    perpendicular distance from the line that the force vector lies on to a given axis (Chapter 10)
    linear mass density
    \(\lambda\), expressed as the number of kilograms of material per meter (Chapter 9)
    linear mass density
    the mass per unit length \(\lambda\) of a one dimensional object (Chapter 10)
    linear wave equation
    equation describing waves that result from a linear restoring force of the medium; any function that is a solution to the wave equation describes a wave moving in the positive x-direction or the negative x-direction with a constant wave speed v (Chapter 16)
    linearity limit (proportionality limit)
    largest stress value beyond which stress is no longer proportional to strain (Chapter 12)
    longitudinal wave
    wave in which the disturbance is parallel to the direction of propagation (Chapter 16)
    loudness
    perception of sound intensity (Chapter 17)

    M¶

    magnitude
    length of a vector (Chapter 2)
    mechanical energy
    sum of the kinetic and potential energies (Chapter 8)
    mechanical wave
    wave that is governed by Newton’s laws and requires a medium (Chapter 16)
    meter
    SI unit for length, abbreviated m (Chapter 1)
    method of adding percents
    the percent uncertainty in a quantity calculated by multiplication or division is the sum of the percent uncertainties in the items used to make the calculation. (Chapter 1)
    metric system
    system in which values can be calculated in factors of 10 (Chapter 1)
    model
    representation of something often too difficult (or impossible) to display directly (Chapter 1)
    moment of inertia
    rotational mass of rigid bodies that relates to how easy or hard it will be to change the angular velocity of the rotating rigid body (Chapter 10)
    momentum
    measure of the quantity of motion that an object has; it takes into account both how fast the object is moving, and its mass; specifically, it is the product of mass and velocity; it is a vector quantity (Chapter 9)

    N¶

    natural angular frequency
    angular frequency of a system oscillating in SHM (Chapter 15)
    neap tide
    low tide created when the Moon and the Sun form a right triangle with Earth (Chapter 13)
    net external force
    vector sum of all external forces acting on an object or system; causes a mass to accelerate (Chapter 5)
    net work
    work done by all the forces acting on an object (Chapter 7)
    neutron star
    most compact object known—outside of a black hole itself (Chapter 13)
    newton
    SI unit of force; 1 N is the force needed to accelerate an object with a mass of 1 kg at a rate of \(1\:\text{m/s}^{2}\) (Chapter 5)
    Newton’s first law of motion
    body at rest remains at rest or, if in motion, remains in motion at constant velocity unless acted on by a net external force; also known as the law of inertia (Chapter 5)
    Newton’s law of gravitation
    every mass attracts every other mass with a force proportional to the product of their masses, inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them, and with direction along the line connecting the center of mass of each (Chapter 13)
    Newton’s second law for rotation
    sum of the torques on a rotating system equals its moment of inertia times its angular acceleration (Chapter 10)
    Newton’s second law of motion
    acceleration of a system is directly proportional to and in the same direction as the net external force acting on the system and is inversely proportional to its mass (Chapter 5)
    Newton’s third law of motion
    whenever one body exerts a force on a second body, the first body experiences a force that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the force that it exerts (Chapter 5)
    node
    point where the string does not move; more generally, nodes are where the wave disturbance is zero in a standing wave (Chapter 16)
    non-conservative force
    force that does work that depends on path (Chapter 8)
    non-Euclidean geometry
    geometry of curved space, describing the relationships among angles and lines on the surface of a sphere, hyperboloid, etc. (Chapter 13)
    non-renewable
    energy source that is not renewable, but is depleted by human consumption (Chapter 8)
    noninertial frame of reference
    accelerated frame of reference (Chapter 6)
    normal force
    force supporting the weight of an object, or a load, that is perpendicular to the surface of contact between the load and its support; the surface applies this force to an object to support the weight of the object (Chapter 5)
    normal mode
    possible standing wave pattern for a standing wave on a string (Chapter 16)
    normal pressure
    pressure of one atmosphere, serves as a reference level for pressure (Chapter 12)
    notes
    basic unit of music with specific names, combined to generate tunes (Chapter 17)
    null vector
    a vector with all its components equal to zero (Chapter 2)

    O¶

    orbital period
    time required for a satellite to complete one orbit (Chapter 13)
    orbital speed
    speed of a satellite in a circular orbit; it can be also be used for the instantaneous speed for noncircular orbits in which the speed is not constant (Chapter 13)
    order of magnitude
    the size of a quantity as it relates to a power of 10 (Chapter 1)
    orthogonal vectors
    two vectors with directions that differ by exactly \(90{}^{\circ}\), synonymous with perpendicular vectors (Chapter 2)
    oscillation
    single fluctuation of a quantity, or repeated and regular fluctuations of a quantity, between two extreme values around an equilibrium or average value (Chapter 15)
    overdamped
    condition in which damping of an oscillator causes it to return to equilibrium without oscillating; oscillator moves more slowly toward equilibrium than in the critically damped system (Chapter 15)
    overtone
    frequency that produces standing waves and is higher than the fundamental frequency (Chapter 16)
    overtones
    all resonant frequencies higher than the fundamental (Chapter 17)

    P¶

    parallel axis
    axis of rotation that is parallel to an axis about which the moment of inertia of an object is known (Chapter 10)
    parallel vectors
    two vectors with exactly the same direction angles (Chapter 2)
    parallel-axis theorem
    if the moment of inertia is known for a given axis, it can be found for any axis parallel to it (Chapter 10)
    parallelogram rule
    geometric construction of the vector sum in a plane (Chapter 2)
    pascal (Pa)
    SI unit of stress, SI unit of pressure (Chapter 12)
    Pascal’s principle
    change in pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to all portions of the fluid and to the walls of its container (Chapter 14)
    percent uncertainty
    the ratio of the uncertainty of a measurement to the measured value, expressed as a percentage (Chapter 1)
    perfectly inelastic
    collision after which all objects are motionless, the final kinetic energy is zero, and the loss of kinetic energy is a maximum (Chapter 9)
    perihelion
    point of closest approach to the Sun of an orbiting body; the corresponding term for the Moon’s closest approach to Earth is the perigee (Chapter 13)
    period (T)
    time taken to complete one oscillation (Chapter 15)
    periodic motion
    motion that repeats itself at regular time intervals (Chapter 15)
    phase shift
    angle, in radians, that is used in a cosine or sine function to shift the function left or right, used to match up the function with the initial conditions of data (Chapter 15)
    phon
    numerical unit of loudness (Chapter 17)
    physical pendulum
    any extended object that swings like a pendulum (Chapter 15)
    physical quantity
    characteristic or property of an object that can be measured or calculated from other measurements (Chapter 1)
    physics
    science concerned with describing the interactions of energy, matter, space, and time; especially interested in what fundamental mechanisms underlie every phenomenon (Chapter 1)
    pitch
    perception of the frequency of a sound (Chapter 17)
    plastic behavior
    material deforms irreversibly, does not go back to its original shape and size when load is removed and stress vanishes (Chapter 12)
    Poiseuille’s law
    rate of laminar flow of an incompressible fluid in a tube: \(Q=\frac{(p_{2}-p_{1})\pi r^{4}}{8\eta l}.\) (Chapter 14)
    Poiseuille’s law for resistance
    resistance to laminar flow of an incompressible fluid in a tube: \(R=\frac{8\eta l}{\pi r^{4}}\) (Chapter 14)
    polar coordinate system
    an orthogonal coordinate system where location in a plane is given by polar coordinates (Chapter 2)
    polar coordinates
    a radial coordinate and an angle (Chapter 2)
    position
    the location of an object at a particular time (Chapter 3)
    position vector
    vector from the origin of a chosen coordinate system to the position of a particle in two- or three-dimensional space (Chapter 4)
    potential energy
    function of position, energy possessed by an object relative to the system considered (Chapter 8)
    potential energy diagram
    graph of a particle’s potential energy as a function of position (Chapter 8)
    potential energy difference
    negative of the work done acting between two points in space (Chapter 8)
    power
    (or instantaneous power) rate of doing work (Chapter 7)
    precession
    circular motion of the pole of the axis of a spinning object around another axis due to a torque (Chapter 11)
    precision
    the degree to which repeated measurements agree with each other (Chapter 1)
    pressure
    force pressing in normal direction on a surface per the surface area, the bulk stress in fluids (Chapter 12)
    pressure
    force per unit area exerted perpendicular to the area over which the force acts (Chapter 14)
    principle of equivalence
    part of the general theory of relativity, it states that there no difference between free fall and being weightless, or a uniform gravitational field and uniform acceleration (Chapter 13)
    projectile motion
    motion of an object subject only to the acceleration of gravity (Chapter 4)
    pulse
    single disturbance that moves through a medium, transferring energy but not mass (Chapter 16)

    R¶

    radial coordinate
    distance to the origin in a polar coordinate system (Chapter 2)
    range
    maximum horizontal distance a projectile travels (Chapter 4)
    reference frame
    coordinate system in which the position, velocity, and acceleration of an object at rest or moving is measured (Chapter 4)
    relative velocity
    velocity of an object as observed from a particular reference frame, or the velocity of one reference frame with respect to another reference frame (Chapter 4)
    renewable
    energy source that is replenished by natural processes, over human time scales (Chapter 8)
    resonance
    large amplitude oscillations in a system produced by a small amplitude driving force, which has a frequency equal to the natural frequency (Chapter 15)
    restoring force
    force acting in opposition to the force caused by a deformation (Chapter 15)
    resultant vector
    vector sum of two (or more) vectors (Chapter 2)
    Reynolds number
    dimensionless parameter that can reveal whether a particular flow is laminar or turbulent (Chapter 14)
    rocket equation
    derived by the Soviet physicist Konstantin Tsiolkovsky in 1897, it gives us the change of velocity that the rocket obtains from burning a mass of fuel that decreases the total rocket mass from \(m_{\text{i}}\) down to m (Chapter 9)
    rolling motion
    combination of rotational and translational motion with or without slipping (Chapter 11)
    rotational dynamics
    analysis of rotational motion using the net torque and moment of inertia to find the angular acceleration (Chapter 10)
    rotational kinetic energy
    kinetic energy due to the rotation of an object; this is part of its total kinetic energy (Chapter 10)
    rotational work
    work done on a rigid body due to the sum of the torques integrated over the angle through with the body rotates (Chapter 10)

    S¶

    scalar
    a number, synonymous with a scalar quantity in physics (Chapter 2)
    scalar component
    a number that multiplies a unit vector in a vector component of a vector (Chapter 2)
    scalar equation
    equation in which the left-hand and right-hand sides are numbers (Chapter 2)
    scalar product
    the result of the scalar multiplication of two vectors is a scalar called a scalar product; also called a dot product (Chapter 2)
    scalar quantity
    quantity that can be specified completely by a single number with an appropriate physical unit (Chapter 2)
    Schwarzschild radius
    critical radius (\(R_{\text{S}}\)) such that if a mass were compressed to the extent that its radius becomes less than the Schwarzschild radius, then the mass will collapse to a singularity, and anything that passes inside that radius cannot escape (Chapter 13)
    second
    the SI unit for time, abbreviated s (Chapter 1)
    second equilibrium condition
    expresses rotational equilibrium; all torques due to external forces acting on the body balance out and their vector sum is zero (Chapter 12)
    shear modulus
    elastic modulus for shear stress (Chapter 12)
    shear strain
    strain caused by shear stress (Chapter 12)
    shear stress
    stress caused by shearing forces (Chapter 12)
    shock wave
    wave front that is produced when a sound source moves faster than the speed of sound (Chapter 17)
    SI units
    the international system of units that scientists in most countries have agreed to use; includes units such as meters, liters, and grams (Chapter 1)
    significant figures
    used to express the precision of a measuring tool used to measure a value (Chapter 1)
    simple harmonic motion (SHM)
    oscillatory motion in a system where the restoring force is proportional to the displacement, which acts in the direction opposite to the displacement (Chapter 15)
    simple harmonic oscillator
    a device that oscillates in SHM where the restoring force is proportional to the displacement and acts in the direction opposite to the displacement (Chapter 15)
    simple pendulum
    point mass, called a pendulum bob, attached to a near massless string (Chapter 15)
    sonic boom
    loud noise that occurs as a shock wave as it sweeps along the ground (Chapter 17)
    sound
    traveling pressure wave that may be periodic; the wave can be modeled as a pressure wave or as an oscillation of molecules (Chapter 17)
    sound intensity level
    unitless quantity telling you the level of the sound relative to a fixed standard (Chapter 17)
    sound pressure level
    ratio of the pressure amplitude to a reference pressure (Chapter 17)
    space-time
    concept of space-time is that time is essentially another coordinate that is treated the same way as any individual spatial coordinate; in the equations that represent both special and general relativity, time appears in the same context as do the spatial coordinates (Chapter 13)
    specific gravity
    ratio of the density of an object to a fluid (usually water) (Chapter 14)
    spring tide
    high tide created when the Moon, the Sun, and Earth are along one line (Chapter 13)
    stable equilibrium point
    point where the net force on a system is zero, but a small displacement of the mass will cause a restoring force that points toward the equilibrium point (Chapter 15)
    standing wave
    wave that can bounce back and forth through a particular region, effectively becoming stationary (Chapter 16)
    static equilibrium
    body is in static equilibrium when it is at rest in our selected inertial frame of reference (Chapter 12)
    static friction
    force that opposes the motion of two systems that are in contact and are not moving relative to each other (Chapter 6)
    strain
    dimensionless quantity that gives the amount of deformation of an object or medium under stress (Chapter 12)
    stress
    quantity that contains information about the magnitude of force causing deformation, defined as force per unit area (Chapter 12)
    stress-strain diagram
    graph showing the relationship between stress and strain, characteristic of a material (Chapter 12)
    superposition
    phenomenon that occurs when two or more waves arrive at the same point (Chapter 16)
    surface mass density
    mass per unit area \(\sigma\) of a two dimensional object (Chapter 10)
    system
    object or collection of objects whose motion is currently under investigation; however, your system is defined at the start of the problem, you must keep that definition for the entire problem (Chapter 9)

    T¶

    tail-to-head geometric construction
    geometric construction for drawing the resultant vector of many vectors (Chapter 2)
    tangential acceleration
    magnitude of which is the time rate of change of speed. Its direction is tangent to the circle. (Chapter 4)
    tensile strain
    strain under tensile stress, given as fractional change in length, which occurs when forces are stretching an object, causing its elongation (Chapter 12)
    tensile stress
    stress caused by tensile forces, only in one direction, which occurs when forces are stretching an object, causing its elongation (Chapter 12)
    tension
    pulling force that acts along a stretched flexible connector, such as a rope or cable (Chapter 5)
    terminal velocity
    constant velocity achieved by a falling object, which occurs when the weight of the object is balanced by the upward drag force (Chapter 6)
    theory
    testable explanation for patterns in nature supported by scientific evidence and verified multiple times by various groups of researchers (Chapter 1)
    theory of general relativity
    Einstein’s theory for gravitation and accelerated reference frames; in this theory, gravitation is the result of mass and energy distorting the space-time around it; it is also often referred to as Einstein’s theory of gravity (Chapter 13)
    thrust
    reaction force that pushes a body forward in response to a backward force (Chapter 5)
    tidal force
    difference between the gravitational force at the center of a body and that at any other location on the body; the tidal force stretches the body (Chapter 13)
    timbre
    number and relative intensity of multiple sound frequencies (Chapter 17)
    time of flight
    elapsed time a projectile is in the air (Chapter 4)
    torque
    cross product of a force and a lever arm to a given axis (Chapter 10)
    torsional pendulum
    any suspended object that oscillates by twisting its suspension (Chapter 15)
    total acceleration
    vector sum of centripetal and tangential accelerations (Chapter 4)
    total displacement
    the sum of individual displacements over a given time period (Chapter 3)
    total linear acceleration
    vector sum of the centripetal acceleration vector and the tangential acceleration vector (Chapter 10)
    trajectory
    path of a projectile through the air (Chapter 4)
    transducer
    device that converts energy of a signal into measurable energy form, for example, a microphone converts sound waves into an electrical signal (Chapter 17)
    transverse wave
    wave in which the disturbance is perpendicular to the direction of propagation (Chapter 16)
    turbulence
    fluid flow in which layers mix together via eddies and swirls (Chapter 14)
    turbulent flow
    type of fluid flow in which layers mix together via eddies and swirls (Chapter 14)
    turning point
    position where the velocity of a particle, in one-dimensional motion, changes sign (Chapter 8)
    two-body pursuit problem
    a kinematics problem in which the unknowns are calculated by solving the kinematic equations simultaneously for two moving objects (Chapter 3)

    U¶

    uncertainty
    a quantitative measure of how much measured values deviate from one another (Chapter 1)
    underdamped
    condition in which damping of an oscillator causes the amplitude of oscillations of a damped harmonic oscillator to decrease over time, eventually approaching zero (Chapter 15)
    unit vector
    vector of a unit magnitude that specifies direction; has no physical unit (Chapter 2)
    unit vectors of the axes
    unit vectors that define orthogonal directions in a plane or in space (Chapter 2)
    units
    standards used for expressing and comparing measurements (Chapter 1)
    universal gravitational constant
    constant representing the strength of the gravitational force, that is believed to be the same throughout the universe (Chapter 13)

    V¶

    vector
    mathematical object with magnitude and direction (Chapter 2)
    vector components
    orthogonal components of a vector; a vector is the vector sum of its vector components. (Chapter 2)
    vector equation

    vector product

    vector sum
    resultant of the combination of two (or more) vectors (Chapter 2)
    velocity vector

    viscosity
    measure of the internal friction in a fluid (Chapter 14)

    W¶
