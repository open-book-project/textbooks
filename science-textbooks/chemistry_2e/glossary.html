    Glossary¶

    The key terms of every chapter, in alphabetical order. Each entry ends with a link to the chapter where the term is defined.

    A¶

    absolute zero
    temperature at which the volume of a gas would be zero according to Charles’s law. (Chapter 9)
    accuracy
    how closely a measurement aligns with a correct value (Chapter 1)
    acid
    substance that produces H3O+ when dissolved in water (Chapter 4)
    acid anhydride
    compound that reacts with water to form an acid or acidic solution (Chapter 18)
    acid ionization
    reaction involving the transfer of a proton from an acid to water, yielding hydronium ions and the conjugate base of the acid (Chapter 14)
    acid ionization constant (Ka)
    equilibrium constant for an acid ionization reaction (Chapter 14)
    acid-base indicator
    weak acid or base whose conjugate partner imparts a different solution color; used in visual assessments of solution pH (Chapter 14)
    acid-base reaction
    reaction involving the transfer of a hydrogen ion between reactant species (Chapter 4)
    acidic
    a solution in which [H3O+] > [OH−] (Chapter 14)
    actinide
    inner transition metal in the bottom of the bottom two rows of the periodic table (Chapter 2)
    actinide series
    (also, actinoid series) actinium and the elements in the second row or the f-block, atomic numbers 89–103 (Chapter 19)
    activated complex
    (also, transition state) unstable combination of reactant species formed during a chemical reaction (Chapter 12)
    activation energy (Ea)
    minimum energy necessary in order for a reaction to take place (Chapter 12)
    active electrode
    electrode that participates as a reactant or product in the oxidation-reduction reaction of an electrochemical cell; the mass of an active electrode changes during the oxidation-reduction reaction (Chapter 17)
    actual yield
    amount of product formed in a reaction (Chapter 4)
    addition reaction
    reaction in which a double carbon-carbon bond forms a single carbon-carbon bond by the addition of a reactant. Typical reaction for an alkene. (Chapter 20)
    adhesive force
    force of attraction between molecules of different chemical identities (Chapter 10)
    alcohol
    organic compound with a hydroxyl group (–OH) bonded to a carbon atom (Chapter 20)
    aldehyde
    organic compound containing a carbonyl group bonded to two hydrogen atoms or a hydrogen atom and a carbon substituent (Chapter 20)
    alkali metal
    element in group 1 (Chapter 2)
    alkaline battery
    primary battery similar to a dry cell that uses an alkaline (often potassium hydroxide) electrolyte; designed to be an improved replacement for the dry cell, but with more energy storage and less electrolyte leakage than typical dry cell (Chapter 17)
    alkaline earth metal
    element in group 2 (Chapter 2)
    alkaline earth metal
    any of the metals (beryllium, magnesium, calcium, strontium, barium, and radium) occupying group 2 of the periodic table; they are reactive, divalent metals that form basic oxides (Chapter 18)
    alkane
    molecule consisting of only carbon and hydrogen atoms connected by single (σ) bonds (Chapter 20)
    alkene
    molecule consisting of carbon and hydrogen containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond (Chapter 20)
    alkyl group
    substituent, consisting of an alkane missing one hydrogen atom, attached to a larger structure (Chapter 20)
    alkyne
    molecule consisting of carbon and hydrogen containing at least one carbon-carbon triple bond (Chapter 20)
    allotropes
    two or more forms of the same element, in the same physical state, with different chemical structures (Chapter 18)
    alloy
    solid mixture of a metallic element and one or more additional elements (Chapter 11)
    alpha (α) decay
    loss of an alpha particle during radioactive decay (Chapter 21)
    alpha particle
    (α or \({}_{2}^{4}\textbf{He}\) or \({}_{2}^{4}\alpha)\) high-energy helium nucleus; a helium atom that has lost two electrons and contains two protons and two neutrons (Chapter 21)
    alpha particle (α particle)
    positively charged particle consisting of two protons and two neutrons (Chapter 2)
    amide
    organic molecule that features a nitrogen atom connected to the carbon atom in a carbonyl group (Chapter 20)
    amine
    organic molecule in which a nitrogen atom is bonded to one or more alkyl group (Chapter 20)
    Amontons’s law
    (also, Gay-Lussac’s law) pressure of a given number of moles of gas is directly proportional to its kelvin temperature when the volume is held constant (Chapter 9)
    amorphous
    solid material such as a glass that does not have a regular repeating component to its three-dimensional structure; a solid but not a crystal (Chapter 18)
    amorphous solid
    (also, noncrystalline solid) solid in which the particles lack an ordered internal structure (Chapter 10)
    amphiphilic
    molecules possessing both hydrophobic (nonpolar) and a hydrophilic (polar) parts (Chapter 11)
    amphiprotic
    species that may either donate or accept a proton in a Brønsted-Lowry acid-base reaction (Chapter 14)
    amphoteric
    species that can act as either an acid or a base (Chapter 14)
    amplitude
    extent of the displacement caused by a wave (Chapter 6)
    analyte
    chemical species of interest (Chapter 4)
    anion
    negatively charged atom or molecule (contains more electrons than protons) (Chapter 2)
    anode
    electrode in an electrochemical cell at which oxidation occurs (Chapter 17)
    antibonding orbital
    molecular orbital located outside of the region between two nuclei; electrons in an antibonding orbital destabilize the molecule (Chapter 8)
    antimatter
    particles with the same mass but opposite properties (such as charge) of ordinary particles (Chapter 21)
    aqueous solution
    solution for which water is the solvent (Chapter 3)
    aromatic hydrocarbon
    cyclic molecule consisting of carbon and hydrogen with delocalized alternating carbon-carbon single and double bonds, resulting in enhanced stability (Chapter 20)
    Arrhenius equation
    mathematical relationship between a reaction’s rate constant, activation energy, and temperature (Chapter 12)
    atmosphere (atm)
    unit of pressure; 1 atm = 101,325 Pa (Chapter 9)
    atom
    smallest particle of an element that can enter into a chemical combination (Chapter 1)
    atomic mass
    average mass of atoms of an element, expressed in amu (Chapter 2)
    atomic mass unit (amu)
    (also, unified atomic mass unit, u, or Dalton, Da) unit of mass equal to \(\frac{1}{12}\) of the mass of a 12C atom (Chapter 2)
    atomic number (Z)
    number of protons in the nucleus of an atom (Chapter 2)
    atomic orbital
    mathematical function that describes the behavior of an electron in an atom (also called the wavefunction) (Chapter 6)
    Aufbau principle
    procedure in which the electron configuration of the elements is determined by “building” them in order of atomic numbers, adding one proton to the nucleus and one electron to the proper subshell at a time (Chapter 6)
    autoionization
    reaction between identical species yielding ionic products; for water, this reaction involves transfer of protons to yield hydronium and hydroxide ions (Chapter 14)
    average rate
    rate of a chemical reaction computed as the ratio of a measured change in amount or concentration of substance to the time interval over which the change occurred (Chapter 12)
    Avogadro’s law
    volume of a gas at constant temperature and pressure is proportional to the number of gas molecules (Chapter 9)
    Avogadro’s number (NA)
    experimentally determined value of the number of entities comprising 1 mole of substance, equal to 6.022 \(\times\) 1023 mol−1 (Chapter 3)
    axial position
    location in a trigonal bipyramidal geometry in which there is another atom at a 180° angle and the equatorial positions are at a 90° angle (Chapter 7)

    B¶

    balanced equation
    chemical equation with equal numbers of atoms for each element in the reactant and product (Chapter 4)
    band of stability
    (also, belt of stability, zone of stability, or valley of stability) region of graph of number of protons versus number of neutrons containing stable (nonradioactive) nuclides (Chapter 21)
    bar
    (bar or b) unit of pressure; 1 bar = 100,000 Pa (Chapter 9)
    barometer
    device used to measure atmospheric pressure (Chapter 9)
    base
    substance that produces OH− when dissolved in water (Chapter 4)
    base anhydride
    metal oxide that behaves as a base towards acids (Chapter 18)
    base ionization
    reaction involving the transfer of a proton from water to a base, yielding hydroxide ions and the conjugate acid of the base (Chapter 14)
    base ionization constant (Kb)
    equilibrium constant for a base ionization reaction (Chapter 14)
    basic
    a solution in which [H3O+] < [OH−] (Chapter 14)
    battery
    single or series of galvanic cells designed for use as a source of electrical power (Chapter 17)
    becquerel (Bq)
    SI unit for rate of radioactive decay; 1 Bq = 1 disintegration/s (Chapter 21)
    beta (β) decay
    breakdown of a neutron into a proton, which remains in the nucleus, and an electron, which is emitted as a beta particle (Chapter 21)
    beta particle
    \(\textbf{(β}\) or \({}_{-1}^{\;0}\textbf{e}\) or \({}_{-1}^{\;0}\beta\textbf{)}\) high-energy electron (Chapter 21)
    bicarbonate anion
    salt of the hydrogen carbonate ion, \(\text{HCO}_{3}{}^{-}\) (Chapter 18)
    bidentate ligand
    ligand that coordinates to one central metal through coordinate bonds from two different atoms (Chapter 19)
    bimolecular reaction
    elementary reaction involving two reactant entities (Chapter 12)
    binary acid
    compound that contains hydrogen and one other element, bonded in a way that imparts acidic properties to the compound (ability to release H+ ions when dissolved in water) (Chapter 2)
    binary compound
    compound containing two different elements. (Chapter 2)
    binding energy per nucleon
    total binding energy for the nucleus divided by the number of nucleons in the nucleus (Chapter 21)
    bismuth
    heaviest member of group 15; a less reactive metal than other representative metals (Chapter 18)
    blackbody
    idealized perfect absorber of all incident electromagnetic radiation; such bodies emit electromagnetic radiation in characteristic continuous spectra called blackbody radiation (Chapter 6)
    body-centered cubic (BCC) solid
    crystalline structure that has a cubic unit cell with lattice points at the corners and in the center of the cell (Chapter 10)
    body-centered cubic unit cell
    simplest repeating unit of a body-centered cubic crystal; it is a cube containing lattice points at each corner and in the center of the cube (Chapter 10)
    Bohr’s model of the hydrogen atom
    structural model in which an electron moves around the nucleus only in circular orbits, each with a specific allowed radius (Chapter 6)
    boiling point
    temperature at which the vapor pressure of a liquid equals the pressure of the gas above it (Chapter 10)
    boiling point elevation
    elevation of the boiling point of a liquid by addition of a solute (Chapter 11)
    boiling point elevation constant
    the proportionality constant in the equation relating boiling point elevation to solute molality; also known as the ebullioscopic constant (Chapter 11)
    bomb calorimeter
    device designed to measure the energy change for processes occurring under conditions of constant volume; commonly used for reactions involving solid and gaseous reactants or products (Chapter 5)
    bond angle
    angle between any two covalent bonds that share a common atom (Chapter 7)
    bond dipole moment
    separation of charge in a bond that depends on the difference in electronegativity and the bond distance represented by partial charges or a vector (Chapter 7)
    bond distance
    (also, bond length) distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms (Chapter 7)
    bond energy
    (also, bond dissociation energy) energy required to break a covalent bond in a gaseous substance (Chapter 7)
    bond length
    distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms at which the lowest potential energy is achieved (Chapter 7)
    bond order
    number of pairs of electrons between two atoms; it can be found by the number of bonds in a Lewis structure or by the difference between the number of bonding and antibonding electrons divided by two (Chapter 8)
    bonding orbital
    molecular orbital located between two nuclei; electrons in a bonding orbital stabilize a molecule (Chapter 8)
    borate
    compound containing boron-oxygen bonds, typically with clusters or chains as a part of the chemical structure (Chapter 18)
    Born-Haber cycle
    thermochemical cycle relating the various energetic steps involved in the formation of an ionic solid from the relevant elements (Chapter 7)
    Boyle’s law
    volume of a given number of moles of gas held at constant temperature is inversely proportional to the pressure under which it is measured (Chapter 9)
    Bragg equation
    equation that relates the angles at which X-rays are diffracted by the atoms within a crystal (Chapter 10)
    Brønsted-Lowry acid
    proton donor (Chapter 14)
    Brønsted-Lowry base
    proton acceptor (Chapter 14)
    buffer
    mixture of appreciable amounts of a weak acid-base pair. The pH of a buffer resists change when small amounts of acid or base are added (Chapter 14)
    buffer capacity
    amount of an acid or base that can be added to a volume of a buffer solution before its pH changes significantly (usually by one pH unit) (Chapter 14)
    buret
    device used for the precise delivery of variable liquid volumes, such as in a titration analysis (Chapter 4)
    π bonding orbital
    molecular orbital formed by side-by-side overlap of atomic orbitals, in which the electron density is found on opposite sides of the internuclear axis (Chapter 8)
    π* bonding orbital
    antibonding molecular orbital formed by out of phase side-by-side overlap of atomic orbitals, in which the electron density is found on both sides of the internuclear axis, and there is a node between the nuclei (Chapter 8)
    σ bonding orbital
    molecular orbital in which the electron density is found along the axis of the bond (Chapter 8)
    σ* bonding orbital
    antibonding molecular orbital formed by out-of-phase overlap of atomic orbital along the axis of the bond, generating a node between the nuclei (Chapter 8)

    C¶

    calorie (cal)
    unit of heat or other energy; the amount of energy required to raise 1 gram of water by 1 degree Celsius; 1 cal is defined as 4.184 J (Chapter 5)
    calorimeter
    device used to measure the amount of heat absorbed or released in a chemical or physical process (Chapter 5)
    calorimetry
    process of measuring the amount of heat involved in a chemical or physical process (Chapter 5)
    capillary action
    flow of liquid within a porous material due to the attraction of the liquid molecules to the surface of the material and to other liquid molecules (Chapter 10)
    carbonate
    salt of the anion \(\text{CO}_{3}{}^{2-};\) often formed by the reaction of carbon dioxide with bases (Chapter 18)
    carbonyl group
    carbon atom double bonded to an oxygen atom (Chapter 20)
    carboxylic acid
    organic compound containing a carbonyl group with an attached hydroxyl group (Chapter 20)
    catalyst
    substance that increases the rate of a reaction without itself being consumed by the reaction (Chapter 12)
    cathode
    electrode in an electrochemical cell at which reduction occurs (Chapter 17)
    cathodic protection
    approach to preventing corrosion of a metal object by connecting it to a sacrificial anode composed of a more readily oxidized metal (Chapter 17)
    cation
    positively charged atom or molecule (contains fewer electrons than protons) (Chapter 2)
    cell notation (schematic)
    symbolic representation of the components and reactions in an electrochemical cell (Chapter 17)
    cell potential (Ecell)
    difference in potential of the cathode and anode half-cells (Chapter 17)
    Celsius (°C)
    unit of temperature; water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C on this scale (Chapter 1)
    central metal
    ion or atom to which one or more ligands is attached through coordinate covalent bonds (Chapter 19)
    chain reaction
    repeated fission caused when the neutrons released in fission bombard other atoms (Chapter 21)
    chalcogen
    element in group 16 (Chapter 2)
    Charles’s law
    volume of a given number of moles of gas is directly proportional to its kelvin temperature when the pressure is held constant (Chapter 9)
    chelate
    complex formed from a polydentate ligand attached to a central metal (Chapter 19)
    chelating ligand
    ligand that attaches to a central metal ion by bonds from two or more donor atoms (Chapter 19)
    chemical change
    change producing a different kind of matter from the original kind of matter (Chapter 1)
    chemical equation
    symbolic representation of a chemical reaction (Chapter 4)
    chemical property
    behavior that is related to the change of one kind of matter into another kind of matter (Chapter 1)
    chemical reduction
    method of preparing a representative metal using a reducing agent (Chapter 18)
    chemical symbol
    one-, two-, or three-letter abbreviation used to represent an element or its atoms (Chapter 2)
    chemical thermodynamics
    area of science that deals with the relationships between heat, work, and all forms of energy associated with chemical and physical processes (Chapter 5)
    chemistry
    study of the composition, properties, and interactions of matter (Chapter 1)
    chemotherapy
    similar to internal radiation therapy, but chemical rather than radioactive substances are introduced into the body to kill cancer cells (Chapter 21)
    chlor-alkali process
    electrolysis process for the synthesis of chlorine and sodium hydroxide (Chapter 18)
    cis configuration
    configuration of a geometrical isomer in which two similar groups are on the same side of an imaginary reference line on the molecule (Chapter 19)
    Clausius-Clapeyron equation
    mathematical relationship between the temperature, vapor pressure, and enthalpy of vaporization for a substance (Chapter 10)
    coefficient
    number placed in front of symbols or formulas in a chemical equation to indicate their relative amount (Chapter 4)
    cohesive force
    force of attraction between identical molecules (Chapter 10)
    colligative property
    property of a solution that depends only on the concentration of a solute species (Chapter 11)
    collision theory
    model that emphasizes the energy and orientation of molecular collisions to explain and predict reaction kinetics (Chapter 12)
    colloid
    (also, colloidal dispersion) mixture in which relatively large solid or liquid particles are dispersed uniformly throughout a gas, liquid, or solid (Chapter 11)
    color-change interval
    range in pH over which the color change of an indicator is observed (Chapter 14)
    combustion analysis
    gravimetric technique used to determine the elemental composition of a compound via the collection and weighing of its gaseous combustion products (Chapter 4)
    combustion reaction
    vigorous redox reaction producing significant amounts of energy in the form of heat and, sometimes, light (Chapter 4)
    common ion effect
    effect on equilibrium when a substance with an ion in common with the dissolved species is added to the solution; causes a decrease in the solubility of an ionic species, or a decrease in the ionization of a weak acid or base (Chapter 15)
    complete ionic equation
    chemical equation in which all dissolved ionic reactants and products, including spectator ions, are explicitly represented by formulas for their dissociated ions (Chapter 4)
    complex ion
    ion consisting of a central atom surrounding molecules or ions called ligands via coordinate covalent bonds (Chapter 15)
    compound
    pure substance that can be decomposed into two or more elements (Chapter 1)
    compressibility factor (Z)
    ratio of the experimentally measured molar volume for a gas to its molar volume as computed from the ideal gas equation (Chapter 9)
    concentrated
    qualitative term for a solution containing solute at a relatively high concentration (Chapter 3)
    concentration
    quantitative measure of the relative amounts of solute and solvent present in a solution (Chapter 3)
    concentration cell
    galvanic cell comprising half-cells of identical composition but for the concentration of one redox reactant or product (Chapter 17)
    condensation
    change from a gaseous to a liquid state (Chapter 10)
    conjugate acid
    substance formed when a base gains a proton (Chapter 14)
    conjugate base
    substance formed when an acid loses a proton (Chapter 14)
    containment system
    (also, shield) a three-part structure of materials that protects the exterior of a nuclear fission reactor and operating personnel from the high temperatures, pressures, and radiation levels inside the reactor (Chapter 21)
    continuous spectrum
    electromagnetic radiation given off in an unbroken series of wavelengths (e.g., white light from the sun) (Chapter 6)
    control rod
    material inserted into the fuel assembly that absorbs neutrons and can be raised or lowered to adjust the rate of a fission reaction (Chapter 21)
    coordinate covalent bond
    (also, dative bond) covalent bond in which both electrons originated from the same atom (Chapter 15)
    coordination compound
    stable compound in which the central metal atom or ion acts as a Lewis acid and accepts one or more pairs of electrons (Chapter 19)
    coordination compound
    substance consisting of atoms, molecules, or ions attached to a central atom through Lewis acid-base interactions (Chapter 19)
    coordination number
    number of atoms closest to any given atom in a crystal or to the central metal atom in a complex (Chapter 10)
    coordination number
    number of coordinate covalent bonds to the central metal atom in a complex or the number of closest contacts to an atom in a crystalline form (Chapter 19)
    coordination sphere
    central metal atom or ion plus the attached ligands of a complex (Chapter 19)
    core electron
    electron in an atom that occupies the orbitals of the inner shells (Chapter 6)
    corrosion
    degradation of metal via a natural electrochemical process (Chapter 17)
    coupled equilibria
    system characterized the simultaneous establishment of two or more equilibrium reactions sharing one or more reactant or product (Chapter 15)
    covalent bond
    attractive force between the nuclei of a molecule’s atoms and pairs of electrons between the atoms (Chapter 2)
    covalent bond
    bond formed when electrons are shared between atoms (Chapter 7)
    covalent compound
    (also, molecular compound) composed of molecules formed by atoms of two or more different elements (Chapter 2)
    covalent network solid
    solid whose particles are held together by covalent bonds (Chapter 10)
    covalent radius
    one-half the distance between the nuclei of two identical atoms when they are joined by a covalent bond (Chapter 6)
    crenation
    process whereby biological cells become shriveled due to loss of water by osmosis (Chapter 11)
    critical mass
    amount of fissionable material that will support a self-sustaining (nuclear fission) chain reaction (Chapter 21)
    critical point
    temperature and pressure above which a gas cannot be condensed into a liquid (Chapter 10)
    crystal field splitting (Δoct)
    difference in energy between the t2g and eg sets or t and e sets of orbitals (Chapter 19)
    crystal field theory
    model that explains the energies of the orbitals in transition metals in terms of electrostatic interactions with the ligands but does not include metal ligand bonding (Chapter 19)
    crystalline solid
    solid in which the particles are arranged in a definite repeating pattern (Chapter 10)
    cubic centimeter (cm3 or cc)
    volume of a cube with an edge length of exactly 1 cm (Chapter 1)
    cubic closest packing (CCP)
    crystalline structure in which planes of closely packed atoms or ions are stacked as a series of three alternating layers of different relative orientations (ABC) (Chapter 10)
    cubic meter (m3)
    SI unit of volume (Chapter 1)
    curie (Ci)
    larger unit for rate of radioactive decay frequently used in medicine; 1 Ci = 3.7 \(\times\) 1010 disintegrations/s (Chapter 21)

    D¶

    d orbital
    region of space with high electron density that is either four lobed or contains a dumbbell and torus shape; describes orbitals with l = 2. (Chapter 6)
    d-block element
    one of the elements in groups 3–11 with valence electrons in d orbitals (Chapter 19)
    Dalton (Da)
    alternative unit equivalent to the atomic mass unit (Chapter 2)
    Dalton’s atomic theory
    set of postulates that established the fundamental properties of atoms (Chapter 2)
    Dalton’s law of partial pressures
    total pressure of a mixture of ideal gases is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of the component gases (Chapter 9)
    daughter nuclide
    nuclide produced by the radioactive decay of another nuclide; may be stable or may decay further (Chapter 21)
    degenerate orbitals
    orbitals that have the same energy (Chapter 6)
    density
    ratio of mass to volume for a substance or object (Chapter 1)
    deposition
    change from a gaseous state directly to a solid state (Chapter 10)
    diamagnetism
    phenomenon in which a material is not magnetic itself but is repelled by a magnetic field; it occurs when there are only paired electrons present (Chapter 8)
    diffraction
    redirection of electromagnetic radiation that occurs when it encounters a physical barrier of appropriate dimensions (Chapter 10)
    diffusion
    movement of an atom or molecule from a region of relatively high concentration to one of relatively low concentration (discussed in this chapter with regard to gaseous species, but applicable to species in any phase) (Chapter 9)
    dilute
    qualitative term for a solution containing solute at a relatively low concentration (Chapter 3)
    dilution
    process of adding solvent to a solution in order to lower the concentration of solutes (Chapter 3)
    dimensional analysis
    (also, factor-label method) versatile mathematical approach that can be applied to computations ranging from simple unit conversions to more complex, multi-step calculations involving several different quantities (Chapter 1)
    dipole moment
    property of a molecule that describes the separation of charge determined by the sum of the individual bond moments based on the molecular structure (Chapter 7)
    dipole-dipole attraction
    intermolecular attraction between two permanent dipoles (Chapter 10)
    diprotic acid
    acid containing two ionizable hydrogen atoms per molecule (Chapter 14)
    diprotic base
    base capable of accepting two protons (Chapter 14)
    dispersed phase
    substance present as relatively large solid or liquid particles in a colloid (Chapter 11)
    dispersion force
    (also, London dispersion force) attraction between two rapidly fluctuating, temporary dipoles; significant only when particles are very close together (Chapter 10)
    dispersion medium
    solid, liquid, or gas in which colloidal particles are dispersed (Chapter 11)
    disproportionation reaction
    chemical reaction where a single reactant is simultaneously reduced and oxidized; it is both the reducing agent and the oxidizing agent (Chapter 18)
    dissociation
    physical process accompanying the dissolution of an ionic compound in which the compound’s constituent ions are solvated and dispersed throughout the solution (Chapter 11)
    dissociation constant
    (Kd) equilibrium constant for the decomposition of a complex ion into its components (Chapter 15)
    dissolved
    describes the process by which solute components are dispersed in a solvent (Chapter 3)
    donor atom
    atom in a ligand with a lone pair of electrons that forms a coordinate covalent bond to a central metal (Chapter 19)
    double bond
    covalent bond in which two pairs of electrons are shared between two atoms (Chapter 7)
    Downs cell
    electrochemical cell used for the commercial preparation of metallic sodium (and chlorine) from molten sodium chloride (Chapter 18)
    dry cell
    primary battery, also called a zinc-carbon battery, based on the spontaneous oxidation of zinc by manganese(IV) (Chapter 17)
    dynamic equilibrium
    state of a system in which reciprocal processes are occurring at equal rates (Chapter 10)

    E¶

    effective nuclear charge
    charge that leads to the Coulomb force exerted by the nucleus on an electron, calculated as the nuclear charge minus shielding (Chapter 6)
    effusion
    transfer of gaseous atoms or molecules from a container to a vacuum through very small openings (Chapter 9)
    eg orbitals
    set of two d orbitals that are oriented on the Cartesian axes for coordination complexes; in octahedral complexes, they are higher in energy than the t2g orbitals (Chapter 19)
    electrode potential (EX)
    the potential of a cell in which the half-cell of interest acts as a cathode when connected to the standard hydrogen electrode (Chapter 17)
    electrolysis
    process using electrical energy to cause a nonspontaneous process to occur (Chapter 17)
    electrolyte
    substance that produces ions when dissolved in water (Chapter 11)
    electrolytic cell
    electrochemical cell in which an external source of electrical power is used to drive an otherwise nonspontaneous process (Chapter 17)
    electromagnetic radiation
    energy transmitted by waves that have an electric-field component and a magnetic-field component (Chapter 6)
    electromagnetic spectrum
    range of energies that electromagnetic radiation can comprise, including radio, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays (Chapter 6)
    electron
    negatively charged, subatomic particle of relatively low mass located outside the nucleus (Chapter 2)
    electron affinity
    energy change associated with addition of an electron to a gaseous atom or ion (Chapter 6)
    electron capture
    combination of a core electron with a proton to yield a neutron within the nucleus (Chapter 21)
    electron configuration
    listing that identifies the electron occupancy of an atom’s shells and subshells (Chapter 6)
    electron density

    high energy outer shell(s) of an atom (Chapter 6)
    valence shell electron-pair repulsion theory (VSEPR)
    theory used to predict the bond angles in a molecule based on positioning regions of high electron density as far apart as possible to minimize electrostatic repulsion (Chapter 7)
    van der Waals equation
    modified version of the ideal gas equation containing additional terms to account for non-ideal gas behavior (Chapter 9)
    van der Waals force
    attractive or repulsive force between molecules, including dipole-dipole, dipole-induced dipole, and London dispersion forces; does not include forces due to covalent or ionic bonding, or the attraction between ions and molecules (Chapter 10)
    van’t Hoff factor (i)
    the ratio of the number of moles of particles in a solution to the number of moles of formula units dissolved in the solution (Chapter 11)
    vapor pressure
    (also, equilibrium vapor pressure) pressure exerted by a vapor in equilibrium with a solid or a liquid at a given temperature (Chapter 10)
    vapor pressure of water
    pressure exerted by water vapor in equilibrium with liquid water in a closed container at a specific temperature (Chapter 9)
    vaporization
    change from liquid state to gaseous state (Chapter 10)
    vector
    quantity having magnitude and direction (Chapter 7)
    viscosity
    measure of a liquid’s resistance to flow (Chapter 10)
    volume
    amount of space occupied by an object (Chapter 1)
    volume percentage
    ratio of solute-to-solution volume expressed as a percentage (Chapter 3)

    W¶

    wave
    oscillation of a property over time or space; can transport energy from one point to another (Chapter 6)
    wave-particle duality
    observation that elementary particles can exhibit both wave-like and particle-like properties (Chapter 6)
    wavefunction (ψ)
    mathematical description of an atomic orbital that describes the shape of the orbital; it can be used to calculate the probability of finding the electron at any given location in the orbital, as well as dynamical variables such as the energy and the angular momentum (Chapter 6)
    wavelength (λ)
    distance between two consecutive peaks or troughs in a wave (Chapter 6)
    weak acid
    acid that reacts only to a slight extent when dissolved in water to yield hydronium ions (Chapter 4)
    weak base
    base that reacts only to a slight extent when dissolved in water to yield hydroxide ions (Chapter 4)
    weak electrolyte
    substance that ionizes only partially when dissolved in water (Chapter 11)
    weak-field ligand
    ligand that causes small crystal field splittings (Chapter 19)
    weight
    force that gravity exerts on an object (Chapter 1)
    work (w)
    energy transfer due to changes in external, macroscopic variables such as pressure and volume; or causing matter to move against an opposing force (Chapter 5)

    X¶

    X-ray crystallography
    experimental technique for determining distances between atoms in a crystal by measuring the angles at which X-rays are diffracted when passing through the crystal (Chapter 10)
