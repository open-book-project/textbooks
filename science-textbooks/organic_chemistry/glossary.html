    Glossary¶

    Key terms used in this book

    A–C¶

    Acetal
    A compound with two OR groups on the same carbon, formed from an aldehyde or ketone and two molecules of alcohol with an acid catalyst. Used to protect carbonyl groups (Chapter 11).
    Activation energy
    The energy difference between the reactants and the transition state of a reaction step; the barrier that must be crossed for the reaction to occur.
    Acyl group
    The group R–C=O. Acyl chlorides, anhydrides, esters and amides are acyl compounds.
    Addition reaction
    A reaction in which two reactants combine to form a single product, as when HBr adds to an alkene.
    Alkoxide
    The conjugate base of an alcohol, RO⁻; a strong base and good nucleophile.
    Anomer
    One of the two stereoisomers of a cyclic sugar that differ only in the configuration at the anomeric carbon (C1 of an aldose); α and β.
    Anti addition
    Addition of two groups to opposite faces of a double bond, as in the addition of bromine to an alkene.
    Anti-periplanar
    An arrangement in which two groups on adjacent carbons lie in the same plane on opposite sides of the C–C bond (dihedral angle 180°). Required in E2 eliminations.
    Arenium ion
    The resonance-stabilised carbocation formed when an electrophile adds to a benzene ring; the intermediate of electrophilic aromatic substitution. Also called a σ complex.
    Aromatic
    Describes a cyclic, planar, fully conjugated system with 4n + 2 π electrons, which has special stability (Hückel's rule).
    Atom economy
    The molar mass of the desired product as a percentage of the total molar mass of all reactants in the balanced equation.
    Axial
    In the chair conformation of cyclohexane, a bond parallel to the axis of the ring, pointing straight up or down.
    Base peak
    The most intense peak in a mass spectrum, assigned a relative abundance of 100.
    Bromonium ion
    A cyclic ion with a positively charged bromine bridging two carbons; the intermediate in the addition of bromine to alkenes.
    Carbanion
    A species with a negatively charged carbon that has three bonds and a lone pair.
    Carbocation
    A species with a positively charged carbon that has three bonds and an empty p orbital; stability increases from methyl to tertiary.
    Chemical shift
    The position of an NMR signal, δ, in parts per million relative to tetramethylsilane.
    Chiral
    Not superimposable on its mirror image.
    Condensation polymer
    A polymer formed from monomers with two functional groups each, with loss of a small molecule (usually water) at each link; polyesters and polyamides.
    Conformation
    One of the shapes a molecule can adopt by rotation about single bonds. Conformations interconvert rapidly and are not isomers.
    Conjugate acid and base
    A pair of species that differ by one proton; when an acid loses a proton it becomes its conjugate base.
    Constitutional isomers
    Isomers that differ in the order in which their atoms are connected.
    Curly arrow
    An arrow showing the movement of a pair of electrons from a lone pair or bond (the tail) to an atom or new bond position (the head). A fish-hook arrow shows one electron.

    D–H¶

    Degree of unsaturation
    The number of rings plus π bonds in a molecule, calculated from the molecular formula as (2C + 2 + N − H − X)/2. Also called double-bond equivalent.
    Diastereomers
    Stereoisomers that are not mirror images of each other.
    E/Z
    Descriptors for the configuration of a double bond: Z when the higher-priority groups (by the CIP rules) are on the same side, E when they are on opposite sides.
    E1 and E2
    Elimination mechanisms: E1 is unimolecular, through a carbocation; E2 is bimolecular and concerted, with the H and leaving group anti-periplanar.
    Electronegativity
    The tendency of an atom in a molecule to attract the electrons of a bond.
    Electrophile
    A species that accepts a pair of electrons to form a new bond; a Lewis acid.
    Elimination reaction
    A reaction in which atoms or groups are removed from adjacent carbons to form a multiple bond.
    Enantiomers
    Stereoisomers that are non-superimposable mirror images of each other.
    Enol
    A tautomer of a carbonyl compound with an OH group on a carbon of a C=C double bond.
    Enolate ion
    The resonance-stabilised anion formed by removing an α-hydrogen from a carbonyl compound; nucleophilic at the α-carbon.
    Equatorial
    In the chair conformation of cyclohexane, a bond pointing outwards from the ring, roughly in its mean plane.
    Formal charge
    The charge assigned to an atom in a Lewis structure, calculated as valence electrons minus non-bonding electrons minus half the bonding electrons.
    Functional group
    A specific arrangement of atoms in a molecule that is responsible for its characteristic reactions.
    Glycosidic bond
    The acetal link joining the anomeric carbon of one sugar to an oxygen of another.
    Grignard reagent
    An organomagnesium halide, RMgX, which behaves as a carbon nucleophile and strong base.
    Hemiacetal
    A compound with an OH and an OR group on the same carbon, formed by adding one molecule of alcohol to an aldehyde or ketone.
    Heterolysis
    Breaking of a covalent bond in which one atom takes both electrons, giving ions.
    Homolysis
    Breaking of a covalent bond in which each atom takes one electron, giving radicals.
    Hybridisation
    The mixing of atomic orbitals on one atom to form hybrid orbitals (sp³, sp², sp) that point in the directions of bonds.
    Hydrogen bond
    An attraction between a hydrogen bonded to N, O or F and a lone pair on another N, O or F atom.
    Hyperconjugation
    Stabilisation of a carbocation or radical by overlap of neighbouring C–H or C–C σ bonds with the empty or half-filled p orbital.

    I–N¶

    Inductive effect
    The polarisation of σ bonds by an electronegative atom or group, transmitted along a chain and falling off with distance.
    Intermediate
    A species formed in one step of a reaction and consumed in a later step, lying in an energy minimum on the reaction profile.
    Isoelectric point
    The pH at which an amino acid or protein carries no net charge.
    Ka and pKa
    The acid dissociation constant and its negative logarithm; a lower pKa means a stronger acid.
    Leaving group
    The atom or group that departs with the bonding electron pair in a substitution or elimination; good leaving groups are weak bases.
    Lewis acid and base
    An electron-pair acceptor and an electron-pair donor.
    Markovnikov's rule
    In electrophilic addition to an unsymmetrical alkene, the electrophile adds so as to form the more stable carbocation; for HX, the hydrogen adds to the carbon that already has more hydrogens.
    Mechanism
    A step-by-step description of a reaction in terms of the movement of electrons and the making and breaking of bonds.
    Meso compound
    A compound that has stereocentres but is achiral because of an internal plane of symmetry.
    Molecular ion
    The radical cation, M⁺•, formed by removing one electron from a molecule in a mass spectrometer; its m/z gives the molar mass.
    n + 1 rule
    In ¹H NMR, a signal from hydrogens with n equivalent neighbouring hydrogens is split into n + 1 lines.
    Newman projection
    A drawing of a molecule viewed along a C–C bond, with the front carbon as a point and the back carbon as a circle.
    Nucleophile
    A species that donates a pair of electrons to form a new bond to an electron-poor atom, usually carbon; a Lewis base.
    Nucleophilic acyl substitution
    The replacement of a leaving group on an acyl carbon by a nucleophile, by addition to give a tetrahedral intermediate followed by elimination.

    O–R¶

    Optical activity
    The rotation of the plane of plane-polarised light by a chiral substance.
    Oxidation (organic)
    An increase in the number of bonds from carbon to more electronegative atoms (O, N, halogens) or a decrease in the number of bonds to hydrogen.
    Peptide bond
    The amide link between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of another.
    Pi (π) bond
    A bond formed by sideways overlap of p orbitals, with electron density above and below the line joining the nuclei.
    Polar aprotic solvent
    A polar solvent without O–H or N–H bonds, such as DMSO or propanone, which speeds SN2 reactions of anions.
    Polymer
    A macromolecule made of many repeating monomer units.
    R/S
    Descriptors for the configuration of a stereocentre, assigned by ranking the groups with the CIP rules and viewing with the lowest priority away: clockwise is R, anticlockwise S.
    Racemic mixture
    A 50:50 mixture of two enantiomers, which is optically inactive.
    Radical
    A species with an unpaired electron.
    Rate-determining step
    The step of a multistep reaction with the highest-energy transition state, which controls the overall rate.
    Rearrangement
    A reaction in which the carbon skeleton or the position of a group changes, such as a hydride or methyl shift in a carbocation.
    Reduction (organic)
    An increase in the number of bonds from carbon to hydrogen or a decrease in the number of bonds to more electronegative atoms.
    Regioselective
    Describes a reaction that could give two or more constitutional isomers but gives mainly one.
    Resonance
    The description of a molecule whose electrons are delocalised by two or more Lewis structures (contributors) that differ only in the positions of electrons; the real molecule is the hybrid.
    Retention factor (Rf)
    In chromatography, the distance moved by a spot divided by the distance moved by the solvent front.

    S–Z¶

    Saponification
    Hydrolysis of an ester by aqueous base, giving a carboxylate salt and an alcohol; used to make soap from fats.
    Sigma (σ) bond
    A bond formed by head-on overlap of orbitals, with electron density concentrated along the line joining the nuclei.
    Skeletal formula
    A drawing in which carbon atoms are vertices and line ends, hydrogens on carbon are omitted, and other atoms are written in.
    SN1 and SN2
    Nucleophilic substitution mechanisms: SN1 is unimolecular, through a carbocation, with racemisation; SN2 is bimolecular and concerted, with backside attack and inversion.
    Stereocentre
    An atom (usually carbon with four different groups) at which interchanging two groups gives a stereoisomer.
    Stereoisomers
    Isomers with the same connectivity that differ in the arrangement of their atoms in space.
    Stereospecific
    Describes a reaction in which different stereoisomers of the reactant give different stereoisomers of the product.
    Substitution reaction
    A reaction in which one atom or group replaces another.
    Syn addition
    Addition of two groups to the same face of a double bond, as in catalytic hydrogenation.
    Tautomers
    Constitutional isomers that interconvert rapidly by movement of a hydrogen atom and a double bond, such as keto and enol forms.
    Torsional strain
    The extra energy of a conformation in which bonds on adjacent atoms are eclipsed.
    Transition state
    The arrangement of highest energy on the path of a reaction step, with bonds partly made and partly broken; it cannot be isolated.
    Zaitsev's rule
    In eliminations, the more substituted (more stable) alkene is usually the main product.
    Zwitterion
    A neutral molecule carrying both a positive and a negative charge, such as an amino acid near neutral pH.
