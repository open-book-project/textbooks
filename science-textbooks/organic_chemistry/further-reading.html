    Further Reading¶

    Standard books and sources for going further

    General textbooks¶

    These comprehensive textbooks cover the whole of this book in more depth, with many more examples and problems. Any recent edition is suitable.

    • J. Clayden, N. Greeves and S. Warren, Organic Chemistry, Oxford University Press. Builds the subject from mechanisms and is especially strong on why reactions happen.
    • P. Y. Bruice, Organic Chemistry, Pearson.
    • J. McMurry, Organic Chemistry, Cengage Learning.
    • K. P. C. Vollhardt and N. E. Schore, Organic Chemistry: Structure and Function, W. H. Freeman.
    • L. G. Wade and J. W. Simek, Organic Chemistry, Pearson.
    • D. R. Klein, Organic Chemistry, Wiley.

    Mechanisms and stereochemistry¶

    • P. Sykes, A Guidebook to Mechanism in Organic Chemistry, Longman. A classic, compact account of reaction mechanisms.
    • E. L. Eliel and S. H. Wilen, Stereochemistry of Organic Compounds, Wiley. The standard advanced reference on stereochemistry and conformational analysis.

    Spectroscopy¶

    • D. L. Pavia, G. M. Lampman, G. S. Kriz and J. R. Vyvyan, Introduction to Spectroscopy, Cengage Learning. A clear introduction to IR, NMR, UV and mass spectrometry with many worked problems.
    • D. H. Williams and I. Fleming, Spectroscopic Methods in Organic Chemistry, McGraw-Hill (later editions Springer). Concise, with extensive tables of data.

    Nomenclature¶

    • International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry, Nomenclature of Organic Chemistry: IUPAC Recommendations and Preferred Names 2013, Royal Society of Chemistry, 2014. The official rules for naming organic compounds.

    Practical work and safety¶

    • B. S. Furniss, A. J. Hannaford, P. W. G. Smith and A. R. Tatchell, Vogel's Textbook of Practical Organic Chemistry, 5th edition, Longman, 1989. The classic laboratory reference for techniques and preparations.
    • United Nations, Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (GHS). The international basis of chemical hazard labels and safety data sheets.

    Green chemistry¶

    • P. T. Anastas and J. C. Warner, Green Chemistry: Theory and Practice, Oxford University Press, 1998. The book that set out the twelve principles.

    Biomolecules¶

    • J. M. Berg, J. L. Tymoczko and L. Stryer, Biochemistry, W. H. Freeman. For the structures and roles of carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids in much greater depth.
