    Glossary¶

    The key terms of every chapter, in alphabetical order. Each entry ends with a link to the chapter where the term is defined.

    A¶

    absentmindedness
    lapses in memory that are caused by breaks in attention or our focus being somewhere else (Chapter 8)
    absolute threshold
    minimum amount of stimulus energy that must be present for the stimulus to be detected 50% of the time (Chapter 5)
    accommodation
    adjustment of a schema by changing a scheme to accommodate new information different from what was already known (Chapter 9)
    acoustic encoding
    input of sounds, words, and music (Chapter 8)
    acquisition
    period of initial learning in classical conditioning in which a human or an animal begins to connect a neutral stimulus and an unconditioned stimulus so that the neutral stimulus will begin to elicit the conditioned response (Chapter 6)
    action potential
    electrical signal that moves down the neuron’s axon (Chapter 3)
    actor-observer bias
    phenomenon of explaining other people’s behaviors are due to internal factors and our own behaviors are due to situational forces (Chapter 12)
    adolescence
    period of development that begins at puberty and ends at early adulthood (Chapter 9)
    adrenal gland
    sits atop our kidneys and secretes hormones involved in the stress response (Chapter 3)
    adrenarche
    maturing of the adrenal glands (Chapter 9)
    advance directive
    a written legal document that details specific interventions a person wants (see living will) (Chapter 9)
    afterimage
    continuation of a visual sensation after removal of the stimulus (Chapter 5)
    ageism
    prejudice and discrimination toward individuals based solely on their age (Chapter 12)
    aggression
    seeking to cause harm or pain to another person (Chapter 12)
    agonist
    drug that mimics or strengthens the effects of a neurotransmitter (Chapter 3)
    agoraphobia
    anxiety disorder characterized by intense fear, anxiety, and avoidance of situations in which it might be difficult to escape if one experiences symptoms of a panic attack (Chapter 15)
    alarm reaction
    first stage of the general adaptation syndrome; characterized as the body’s immediate physiological reaction to a threatening situation or some other emergency; analogous to the fight-or-flight response (Chapter 14)
    algorithm
    problem-solving strategy characterized by a specific set of instructions (Chapter 7)
    all-or-none
    phenomenon that incoming signal from another neuron is either sufficient or insufficient to reach the threshold of excitation (Chapter 3)
    allele
    specific version of a gene (Chapter 3)
    alpha wave
    type of brain wave characteristic during the early part of NREM stage 1 sleep, which has fairly low amplitude and a frequency of 8–12 Hz (Chapter 4)
    altruism
    humans’ desire to help others even if the costs outweigh the benefits of helping (Chapter 12)
    American Psychological Association (APA)
    professional organization representing psychologists in the United States (Chapter 1)
    Americans with Disabilities Act
    employers cannot discriminate against any individual based on a disability (Chapter 13)
    amnesia
    loss of long-term memory that occurs as the result of disease, physical trauma, or psychological trauma (Chapter 8)
    amplitude
    height of a wave (Chapter 5)
    amygdala
    structure in the limbic system involved in our experience of emotion and tying emotional meaning to our memories (Chapter 3)
    anal stage
    psychosexual stage in which children experience pleasure in their bowel and bladder movements (Chapter 11)
    analytical intelligence
    aligned with academic problem solving and computations (Chapter 7)
    analytical psychology
    Jung’s theory focusing on the balance of opposing forces within one’s personality and the significance of the collective unconscious (Chapter 11)
    anchoring bias
    faulty heuristic in which you fixate on a single aspect of a problem to find a solution (Chapter 7)
    anorexia nervosa
    eating disorder characterized by an individual maintaining body weight that is well below average through starvation and/or excessive exercise (Chapter 10)
    antagonist
    drug that blocks or impedes the normal activity of a given neurotransmitter (Chapter 3)
    anterograde amnesia
    loss of memory for events that occur after the brain trauma (Chapter 8)
    antisocial personality disorder
    characterized by a lack of regard for others’ rights, impulsivity, deceitfulness, irresponsibility, and lack of remorse over misdeeds (Chapter 15)
    anxiety disorder
    characterized by excessive and persistent fear and anxiety, and by related disturbances in behavior (Chapter 15)
    archetype
    pattern that exists in our collective unconscious across cultures and societies (Chapter 11)
    archival research
    method of research using past records or data sets to answer various research questions, or to search for interesting patterns or relationships (Chapter 2)
    arousal theory
    strong emotions trigger the formation of strong memories and weaker emotional experiences form weaker memories (Chapter 8)
    artificial concept
    concept that is defined by a very specific set of characteristics (Chapter 7)
    Asch effect
    group majority influences an individual’s judgment, even when that judgment is inaccurate (Chapter 12)
    assimilation
    adjustment of a schema by adding information similar to what is already known (Chapter 9)
    associative learning
    form of learning that involves connecting certain stimuli or events that occur together in the environment (classical and operant conditioning) (Chapter 6)
    asthma
    psychophysiological disorder in which the airways of the respiratory system become obstructed, leading to great difficulty expelling air from the lungs (Chapter 14)
    asylum
    institution created for the specific purpose of housing people with psychological disorders (Chapter 16)
    Atkinson-Shiffrin model
    memory model that states we process information through three systems: sensory memory, short-term memory, and long-term memory (Chapter 8)
    attachment
    long-standing connection or bond with others (Chapter 9)
    attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder
    childhood disorder characterized by inattentiveness and/or hyperactive, impulsive behavior (Chapter 15)
    attitude
    evaluations of or feelings toward a person, idea, or object that are typically positive or negative (Chapter 12)
    attribution
    explanation for the behavior of other people (Chapter 12)
    attrition
    reduction in number of research participants as some drop out of the study over time (Chapter 2)
    atypical
    describes behaviors or feelings that deviate from the norm (Chapter 15)
    auditory cortex
    strip of cortex in the temporal lobe that is responsible for processing auditory information (Chapter 3)
    authoritarian parenting style
    parents place a high value on conformity and obedience, are often rigid, and express little warmth to the child (Chapter 9)
    authoritative parenting style
    parents give children reasonable demands and consistent limits, express warmth and affection, and listen to the child’s point of view (Chapter 9)
    autism spectrum disorder
    childhood disorder characterized by deficits in social interaction and communication, and repetitive patterns of behavior or interests (Chapter 15)
    automatic processing
    encoding of informational details like time, space, frequency, and the meaning of words (Chapter 8)
    autonomic nervous system
    controls our internal organs and glands (Chapter 3)
    availability heuristic
    faulty heuristic in which you make a decision based on information readily available to you (Chapter 7)
    aversive conditioning
    counterconditioning technique that pairs an unpleasant stimulant with an undesirable behavior (Chapter 16)
    avoidant attachment
    characterized by child’s unresponsiveness to parent, does not use the parent as a secure base, and does not care if parent leaves (Chapter 9)
    axon
    major extension of the soma (Chapter 3)

    B¶

    bariatric surgery
    type of surgery that modifies the gastrointestinal system to reduce the amount of food that can be eaten and/or limiting how much of the digested food can be absorbed (Chapter 10)
    basilar membrane
    thin strip of tissue within the cochlea that contains the hair cells which serve as the sensory receptors for the auditory system (Chapter 5)
    basolateral complex
    part of the brain with dense connections with a variety of sensory areas of the brain; it is critical for classical conditioning and attaching emotional value to memory (Chapter 10)
    behavior therapy
    therapeutic orientation that employs principles of learning to help clients change undesirable behaviors (Chapter 16)
    behaviorism
    focus on observing and controlling behavior (Chapter 1)
    beta wave
    type of brain wave characteristic during wakefulness, which has a very low amplitude and a frequency of 13–30 Hz (Chapter 4)
    bias
    how feelings and view of the world distort memory of past events (Chapter 8)
    binaural cue
    two-eared cue to localize sound (Chapter 5)
    binge eating disorder
    type of eating disorder characterized by binge eating and associated distress (Chapter 10)
    binocular cue
    cue that relies on the use of both eyes (Chapter 5)
    binocular disparity
    slightly different view of the world that each eye receives (Chapter 5)
    biofeedback
    stress-reduction technique using electronic equipment to measure a person’s involuntary (neuromuscular and autonomic) activity and provide feedback to help the person gain a level of voluntary control over these processes (Chapter 14)
    biological perspective
    view that psychological disorders like depression and schizophrenia are associated with imbalances in one or more neurotransmitter systems (Chapter 3)
    biological rhythm
    internal cycle of biological activity (Chapter 4)
    biomedical therapy
    treatment that involves medication and/or medical procedures to treat psychological disorders (Chapter 16)
    biopsychology
    study of how biology influences behavior (Chapter 1)
    biopsychosocial model
    perspective that asserts that biology, psychology, and social factors interact to determine an individual’s health (Chapter 1)
    bipolar and related disorders
    group of mood disorders in which mania is the defining feature (Chapter 15)
    bipolar disorder
    mood disorder characterized by mood states that vacillate between depression and mania (Chapter 15)
    bisexual
    emotional, romantic, and/or erotic attraction to those of the same gender or to those of another gender (Chapter 10)
    blind spot
    point where we cannot respond to visual information in that portion of the visual field (Chapter 5)
    blocking
    memory error in which you cannot access stored information (Chapter 8)
    body dysmorphic disorder
    involves excessive preoccupation with an imagined defect in physical appearance (Chapter 15)
    body language
    emotional expression through body position or movement (Chapter 10)
    bona fide occupational qualification (BFOQ)
    requirement of certain occupations for which denying an individual employment would otherwise violate the law, such as requirements concerning religion or sex (Chapter 13)
    borderline personality disorder
    instability in interpersonal relationships, self-image, and mood, as well as impulsivity; key features include intolerance of being alone and fear of abandonment, unstable relationships, unpredictable behavior and moods, and intense and inappropriate anger (Chapter 15)
    bottom-up processing
    system in which perceptions are built from sensory input (Chapter 5)
    Broca’s area
    region in the left hemisphere that is essential for language production (Chapter 3)
    bulimia nervosa
    type of eating disorder characterized by binge eating followed by purging (Chapter 10)
    bullying
    a person, often an adolescent, being treated negatively repeatedly and over time (Chapter 12)
    bystander effect
    situation in which a witness or bystander does not volunteer to help a victim or person in distress (Chapter 12)

    C¶

    Cannon-Bard theory of emotion
    physiological arousal and emotional experience occur at the same time (Chapter 10)
    cardiovascular disorders
    disorders that involve the heart and blood circulation system (Chapter 14)
    cataplexy
    lack of muscle tone or muscle weakness, and in some cases complete paralysis of the voluntary muscles (Chapter 4)
    catatonic behavior
    decreased reactivity to the environment; includes posturing and catatonic stupor (Chapter 15)
    cause-and-effect relationship
    changes in one variable cause the changes in the other variable; can be determined only through an experimental research design (Chapter 2)
    central nervous system (CNS)
    brain and spinal cord (Chapter 3)
    central nucleus
    part of the brain involved in attention and has connections with the hypothalamus and various brainstem areas to regulate the autonomic nervous and endocrine systems’ activity (Chapter 10)
    central route persuasion
    logic-driven arguments using data and facts to convince people of an argument’s worthiness (Chapter 12)
    central sleep apnea
    sleep disorder with periods of interrupted breathing due to a disruption in signals sent from the brain that regulate breathing (Chapter 4)
    cerebellum
    hindbrain structure that controls our balance, coordination, movement, and motor skills, and it is thought to be important in processing some types of memory (Chapter 3)
    cerebral cortex
    surface of the brain that is associated with our highest mental capabilities (Chapter 3)
    checklist
    method used to reduce workplace accidents (Chapter 13)
    chromosome
    long strand of genetic information (Chapter 3)
    chunking
    organizing information into manageable bits or chunks (Chapter 8)
    circadian rhythm
    biological rhythm that occurs over approximately 24 hours (Chapter 4)
    classical conditioning
    learning in which the stimulus or experience occurs before the behavior and then gets paired or associated with the behavior (Chapter 6)
    clinical or case study
    observational research study focusing on one or a few people (Chapter 2)
    clinical psychology
    area of psychology that focuses on the diagnosis and treatment of psychological disorders and other problematic patterns of behavior (Chapter 1)
    closure
    organizing our perceptions into complete objects rather than as a series of parts (Chapter 5)
    cochlea
    fluid-filled, snail-shaped structure that contains the sensory receptor cells of the auditory system (Chapter 5)
    cochlear implant
    electronic device that consists of a microphone, a speech processor, and an electrode array to directly stimulate the auditory nerve to transmit information to the brain (Chapter 5)
    codeine
    opiate with relatively low potency often prescribed for minor pain (Chapter 4)
    cognition
    thinking, including perception, learning, problem solving, judgment, and memory (Chapter 7)
    cognitive development
    domain of lifespan development that examines learning, attention, memory, language, thinking, reasoning, and creativity (Chapter 9)
    cognitive dissonance
    psychological discomfort that arises from a conflict in a person’s behaviors, attitudes, or beliefs that runs counter to one’s positive self-perception (Chapter 12)
    cognitive empathy
    ability to take the perspective of others and to feel concern for others (Chapter 9)
    cognitive map
    mental picture of the layout of the environment (Chapter 6)
    cognitive psychology
    study of cognitions, or thoughts, and their relationship to experiences and actions (Chapter 1)
    cognitive psychology
    field of psychology dedicated to studying every aspect of how people think (Chapter 7)
    cognitive script
    set of behaviors that are performed the same way each time; also referred to as an event schema (Chapter 7)
    cognitive therapy
    form of psychotherapy that focuses on how a person’s thoughts lead to feelings of distress, with the aim of helping them change these irrational thoughts (Chapter 16)
    cognitive-behavioral therapy
    psychotherapy that focuses on cognitive processes and problem behaviors that is sometimes used to treat sleep disorders such as insomnia (Chapter 4)
    cognitive-behavioral therapy
    form of psychotherapy that aims to change cognitive distortions and self-defeating behaviors (Chapter 16)
    cognitive-mediational theory
    our emotions are determined by our appraisal of the stimulus (Chapter 10)
    collective unconscious
    theoretical repository of information shared by all people across cultures, as described by Carl Jung (Chapter 4)
    collective unconscious
    common psychological tendencies that have been passed down from one generation to the next (Chapter 11)
    collectivist culture
    culture that focuses on communal relationships with others such as family, friends, and community (Chapter 12)
    comorbid disorder
    individual who has two or more diagnoses, which often includes a substance abuse diagnosis and another psychiatric diagnosis, such as depression, bipolar disorder, or schizophrenia (Chapter 16)
    comorbidity
    co-occurrence of two disorders in the same individual (Chapter 15)
    companionate love
    type of love consisting of intimacy and commitment, but not passion; associated with close friendships and family relationships (Chapter 12)
    components of emotion
    physiological arousal, psychological appraisal, and subjective experience (Chapter 10)
    computerized tomography (CT) scan
    imaging technique in which a computer coordinates and integrates multiple x-rays of a given area (Chapter 3)
    concept
    category or grouping of linguistic information, objects, ideas, or life experiences (Chapter 7)
    conception
    when a sperm fertilizes an egg and forms a zygote (Chapter 9)
    concrete operational stage
    third stage in Piaget’s theory of cognitive development; from about 7 to 11 years old, children can think logically about real (concrete) events (Chapter 9)
    conditioned response (CR)
    response caused by the conditioned stimulus (Chapter 6)
    conditioned stimulus (CS)
    stimulus that elicits a response due to its being paired with an unconditioned stimulus (Chapter 6)
    conductive hearing loss
    failure in the vibration of the eardrum and/or movement of the ossicles (Chapter 5)
    cone
    specialized photoreceptor that works best in bright light conditions and detects color (Chapter 5)
    confederate
    person who works for a researcher and is aware of the experiment, but who acts as a participant; used to manipulate social situations as part of the research design (Chapter 12)
    confidentiality
    therapist cannot disclose confidential communications to any third party, unless mandated or permitted by law (Chapter 16)
    confirmation bias
    tendency to ignore evidence that disproves ideas or beliefs (Chapter 2)
    confirmation bias
    faulty heuristic in which you focus on information that confirms your beliefs (Chapter 7)
    confirmation bias
    seeking out information that supports our stereotypes while ignoring information that is inconsistent with our stereotypes (Chapter 12)
    conformity
    when individuals change their behavior to go along with the group even if they do not agree with the group (Chapter 12)
    confounding variable
    unanticipated outside factor that affects both variables of interest, often giving the false impression that changes in one variable causes changes in the other variable, when, in actuality, the outside factor causes changes in both variables (Chapter 2)
    congenital deafness
    deafness from birth (Chapter 5)
    congenital insensitivity to pain (congenital analgesia)
    genetic disorder that results in the inability to experience pain (Chapter 5)
    congruence
    state of being in which our thoughts about our real and ideal selves are very similar (Chapter 11)
    conscious
    mental activity (thoughts, feelings, and memories) that we can access at any time (Chapter 11)
    consciousness
    awareness of internal and external stimuli (Chapter 4)
    conservation
    idea that even if you change the appearance of something, it is still equal in size, volume, or number as long as nothing is added or removed (Chapter 9)
    construction
    formulation of new memories (Chapter 8)
    consummate love
    type of love occurring when intimacy, passion, and commitment are all present (Chapter 12)
    Contemporized-Themes Concerning Blacks Test (C-TCB)
    projective test designed to be culturally relevant to African Americans, using images that relate to African-American culture (Chapter 11)
    continuous development
    view that development is a cumulative process: gradually improving on existing skills (Chapter 9)
    continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP)
    device used to treat sleep apnea; includes a mask that fits over the sleeper’s nose and mouth, which is connected to a pump that pumps air into the person’s airways, forcing them to remain open (Chapter 4)
    continuous reinforcement
    rewarding a behavior every time it occurs (Chapter 6)
    control group
    serves as a basis for comparison and controls for chance factors that might influence the results of the study—by holding such factors constant across groups so that the experimental manipulation is the only difference between groups (Chapter 2)
    convergent thinking
    providing correct or established answers to problems (Chapter 7)
    coping
    mental or behavioral efforts used to manage problems relating to stress, including its cause and the unpleasant feelings and emotions it produces (Chapter 14)
    cornea
    transparent covering over the eye (Chapter 5)
    corpus callosum
    thick band of neural fibers connecting the brain’s two hemispheres (Chapter 3)
    correlation
    relationship between two or more variables; when two variables are correlated, one variable changes as the other does (Chapter 2)
    correlation coefficient
    number from -1 to +1, indicating the strength and direction of the relationship between variables, and usually represented by r (Chapter 2)
    cortisol
    stress hormone released by the adrenal glands when encountering a stressor; helps to provide a boost of energy, thereby preparing the individual to take action (Chapter 14)
    counseling psychology
    area of psychology that focuses on improving emotional, social, vocational, and other aspects of the lives of psychologically healthy individuals (Chapter 1)
    counterconditioning
    classical conditioning therapeutic technique in which a client learns a new response to a stimulus that has previously elicited an undesirable behavior (Chapter 16)
    couples therapy
    two people in an intimate relationship, such as husband and wife, who are having difficulties and are trying to resolve them with therapy (Chapter 16)
    creative intelligence
    ability to produce new products, ideas, or inventing a new, novel solution to a problem (Chapter 7)
    creativity
    ability to generate, create, or discover new ideas, solutions, and possibilities (Chapter 7)
    critical (sensitive) period
    time during fetal growth when specific parts or organs develop (Chapter 9)
    cross-sectional research
    compares multiple segments of a population at a single time (Chapter 2)
    crystallized intelligence
    characterized by acquired knowledge and the ability to retrieve it (Chapter 7)
    cultural competence
    therapist’s understanding and attention to issues of race, culture, and ethnicity in providing treatment (Chapter 16)
    cultural display rule
    one of the culturally specific standards that govern the types and frequencies of emotions that are acceptable (Chapter 10)
    cultural intelligence
    ability with which people can understand and relate to those in another culture (Chapter 7)
    culture
    all of the beliefs, customs, art, and traditions of a particular society (Chapter 11)
    cyberbullying
    repeated behavior that is intended to cause psychological or emotional harm to another person and that takes place online (Chapter 12)

    D¶

    daily hassles
    minor irritations and annoyances that are part of our everyday lives and are capable of producing stress (Chapter 14)
    deafness
    partial or complete inability to hear (Chapter 5)
    debriefing
    when an experiment involved deception, participants are told complete and truthful information about the experiment at its conclusion (Chapter 2)
    deception
    purposely misleading experiment participants in order to maintain the integrity of the experiment (Chapter 2)
    decibel (dB)
    logarithmic unit of sound intensity (Chapter 5)
    declarative memory
    type of long-term memory of facts and events we personally experience (Chapter 8)
    deductive reasoning
    results are predicted based on a general premise (Chapter 2)
    defense mechanism
    unconscious protective behaviors designed to reduce ego anxiety (Chapter 11)
    deinstitutionalization
    process of closing large asylums and integrating people back into the community where they can be treated locally (Chapter 16)
    delta wave
    type of brain wave characteristic during stage 3 NREM sleep, which has a high amplitude and low frequency of less than 3 Hz (Chapter 4)
    delusion
    belief that is contrary to reality and is firmly held, despite contradictory evidence (Chapter 15)
    dendrite
    branch-like extension of the soma that receives incoming signals from other neurons (Chapter 3)
    deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA)
    helix-shaped molecule made of nucleotide base pairs (Chapter 3)
    dependent variable
    variable that the researcher measures to see how much effect the independent variable had (Chapter 2)
    depersonalization/derealization disorder
    dissociative disorder in which people feel detached from the self (depersonalization), and the world feels artificial and unreal (derealization) (Chapter 15)
    depressant
    drug that tends to suppress central nervous system activity (Chapter 4)
    depressive disorder
    one of a group of mood disorders in which depression is the defining feature (Chapter 15)
    depth perception
    ability to perceive depth (Chapter 5)
    developmental milestone
    approximate ages at which children reach specific normative events (Chapter 9)
    developmental psychology
    scientific study of development across a lifespan (Chapter 1)
    diabetes
    disease related to insufficient insulin production (Chapter 3)
    diagnosis
    determination of which disorder a set of symptoms represents (Chapter 15)
    Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5)
    authoritative index of mental disorders and the criteria for their diagnosis; published by the American Psychiatric Association (APA) (Chapter 15)
    diathesis-stress model
    suggests that people with a predisposition for a disorder (a diathesis) are more likely to develop the disorder when faced with stress; model of psychopathology (Chapter 15)
    diffusion of responsibility
    tendency for no one in a group to help because the responsibility to help is spread throughout the group (Chapter 12)
    discontinuous development
    view that development takes place in unique stages, which happen at specific times or ages (Chapter 9)
    discrimination
    negative actions toward individuals as a result of their membership in a particular group (Chapter 12)
    disorganized attachment
    characterized by the child’s odd behavior when faced with the parent; type of attachment seen most often with kids that are abused (Chapter 9)
    disorganized thinking
    disjointed and incoherent thought processes, usually detected by what a person says (Chapter 15)
    disorganized/abnormal motor behavior
    highly unusual behaviors and movements (such as child-like behaviors), repeated and purposeless movements, and displaying odd facial expressions and gestures (Chapter 15)
    displacement
    ego defense mechanism in which a person transfers inappropriate urges or behaviors toward a more acceptable or less threatening target (Chapter 11)
    dispositionism
    describes a perspective common to personality psychologists, which asserts that our behavior is determined by internal factors, such as personality traits and temperament (Chapter 12)
    dissertation
    long research paper about research that was conducted as a part of the candidate’s doctoral training (Chapter 1)
    dissociative amnesia
    dissociative disorder characterized by an inability to recall important personal information, usually following an extremely stressful or traumatic experience (Chapter 15)
    dissociative disorders
    group of DSM-5 disorders in which the primary feature is that a person becomes dissociated, or split off, from their core sense of self, resulting in disturbances in identity and memory (Chapter 15)
    dissociative fugue
    symptom of dissociative amnesia in which a person suddenly wanders away from one’s home and experiences confusion about their identity (Chapter 15)
    dissociative identity disorder
    dissociative disorder (formerly known as multiple personality disorder) in which a person exhibits two or more distinct, well-defined personalities or identities and experiences memory gaps for the time during which another identity emerged (Chapter 15)
    distorted body image
    individuals view themselves as overweight even though they are not (Chapter 10)
    distress
    bad form of stress; usually high in intensity; often leads to exhaustion, fatigue, feeling burned out; associated with erosions in performance and health (Chapter 14)
    divergent thinking
    ability to think “outside the box” to arrive at novel solutions to a problem (Chapter 7)
    diversity training
    training employees about cultural differences with the goal of improving teamwork (Chapter 13)
    do not resuscitate (DNR)
    a legal document stating that if a person stops breathing or their heart stops, medical personnel such as doctors and nurses are not to take steps to revive or resuscitate the patient (Chapter 9)
    dominant allele
    allele whose phenotype will be expressed in an individual that possesses that allele (Chapter 3)
    dopamine hypothesis
    theory of schizophrenia that proposes that an overabundance of dopamine or dopamine receptors is responsible for the onset and maintenance of schizophrenia (Chapter 15)
    double-blind study
    experiment in which both the researchers and the participants are blind to group assignments (Chapter 2)
    downsizing
    process in which an organization tries to achieve greater overall efficiency by reducing the number of employees (Chapter 13)
    dream analysis
    technique in psychoanalysis in which patients recall their dreams and the psychoanalyst interprets them to reveal unconscious desires or struggles (Chapter 16)
    drive theory
    deviations from homeostasis create physiological needs that result in psychological drive states that direct behavior to meet the need and ultimately bring the system back to homeostasis (Chapter 10)
    dyscalculia
    learning disability that causes difficulty in learning or comprehending mathematics (Chapter 7)
    dysgraphia
    learning disability that causes extreme difficulty in writing legibly (Chapter 7)
    dyslexia
    common learning disability in which letters are not processed properly by the brain (Chapter 7)

    E¶

    effortful processing
    encoding of information that takes effort and attention (Chapter 8)
    ego
    aspect of personality that represents the self, or the part of one’s personality that is visible to others (Chapter 11)
    egocentrism
    preoperational child’s difficulty in taking the perspective of others (Chapter 9)
    elaborative rehearsal
    thinking about the meaning of new information and its relation to knowledge already stored in your memory (Chapter 8)
    electroconvulsive therapy (ECT)
    type of biomedical therapy that involves using an electrical current to induce seizures in a person to help alleviate the effects of severe depression (Chapter 16)
    electroencephalography (EEG)
    recording the electrical activity of the brain via electrodes on the scalp (Chapter 3)
    electromagnetic spectrum
    all the electromagnetic radiation that occurs in our environment (Chapter 5)
    embryo
    multi-cellular organism in its early stages of development (Chapter 9)
    emerging adulthood
    newly defined period of lifespan development from 18 years old to the mid-20s; young people are taking longer to complete college, get a job, get married, and start a family (Chapter 9)
    emotion
    subjective state of being often described as feelings (Chapter 10)
    emotional intelligence
    ability to understand emotions and motivations in yourself and others (Chapter 7)
    empathy
    capacity to understand another person’s perspective—to feel what they feel (Chapter 12)
    empirical
    grounded in objective, tangible evidence that can be observed time and time again, regardless of who is observing (Chapter 2)
    empirical method
    method for acquiring knowledge based on observation, including experimentation, rather than a method based only on forms of logical argument or previous authorities (Chapter 1)
    encoding
    input of information into the memory system (Chapter 8)
    endocrine system
    series of glands that produce chemical substances known as hormones (Chapter 3)
    engram
    physical trace of memory (Chapter 8)
    epigenetics
    study of gene-environment interactions, such as how the same genotype leads to different phenotypes (Chapter 3)
