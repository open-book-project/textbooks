    Glossary¶

    The key terms of every chapter, in alphabetical order. Each entry ends with a link to the chapter where the term is defined.

    A¶

    absolute monarchies
    governments wherein a monarch has absolute or unmitigated power (Chapter 17)
    absolute poverty
    deprivation so severe that it puts day-to-day survival in jeopardy. (Chapter 9)
    accuracy
    using a tool makes the measuring more precise. (Chapter 2)
    achieved status
    the status a person chooses, such as a level of education or income (Chapter 4)
    acting crowds
    crowds of people who are focused on a specific action or goal (Chapter 21)
    activity theory
    a theory which suggests that for individuals to enjoy old age and feel satisfied, they must maintain activities and find a replacement for the statuses and associated roles they have left behind as they aged (Chapter 13)
    age stratification theory
    a theory which states that members of society are stratified by age, just as they are stratified by race, class, and gender (Chapter 13)
    ageism
    discrimination based on age (Chapter 13)
    aggregate
    a collection of people who exist in the same place at the same time, but who don’t interact or share a sense of identity (Chapter 6)
    agricultural societies
    societies that rely on farming as a way of life (Chapter 4)
    alienation
    an individual’s isolation from his society, his work, and his sense of self (Chapter 4)
    alternative movements
    social movements that limit themselves to self-improvement changes in individuals (Chapter 21)
    amalgamation
    the process by which a minority group and a majority group combine to form a new group (Chapter 11)
    ambilineal
    a type of unilateral descent that follows either the father’s or the mother’s side exclusively (Chapter 14)
    anarchy
    the absence of any organized government (Chapter 17)
    animism
    the religion that believes in the divinity of nonhuman beings, like animals, plants, and objects of the natural world (Chapter 15)
    anomie
    a situation in which society no longer has the support of a firm collective consciousness (Chapter 4)
    anticipatory socialization
    the way we prepare for future life roles (Chapter 5)
    antipositivism
    the view that social researchers should strive for subjectivity as they worked to represent social processes, cultural norms, and societal values (Chapter 1)
    antiracist
    a person who opposes racism and acts for racial justice (Chapter 11)
    anxiety disorders
    feelings of worry and fearfulness that last for months at a time (Chapter 19)
    ascribed status
    the status outside of an individual’s control, such as sex or race (Chapter 4)
    assembling perspective
    a theory that credits individuals in crowds as behaving as rational thinkers and views crowds as engaging in purposeful behavior and collective action (Chapter 21)
    assimilation
    the process by which a minority individual or group takes on the characteristics of the dominant culture (Chapter 11)
    asylum-seekers
    those whose claim to refugee status have not been validated (Chapter 20)
    atheism
    the belief in no deities (Chapter 15)
    authoritarian leader
    a leader who issues orders and assigns tasks (Chapter 6)
    authority
    power that people accept because it comes from a source that is perceived as legitimate (Chapter 17)
    automation
    workers being replaced by technology (Chapter 18)

    B¶

    baby Boomers
    people in the United States born between approximately 1946 and 1964 (Chapter 13)
    bartering
    a process where people exchange one form of goods or services for another (Chapter 18)
    beliefs
    tenets or convictions that people hold to be true (Chapter 3)
    bigamy
    the act of entering into marriage while still married to another person (Chapter 14)
    bilateral descent
    the tracing of kinship through both parents’ ancestral lines (Chapter 14)
    biological determinism
    the belief that men and women behave differently due to inherent sex differences related to their biology (Chapter 12)
    bourgeoisie
    the owners of the means of production in a society (Chapter 4)
    bureaucracies
    formal organizations characterized by a hierarchy of authority, a clear division of labor, explicit rules, and impersonality. (Chapter 6)

    C¶

    cancer cluster
    a geographic area with high levels of cancer within its population (Chapter 20)
    capital flight
    the movement (flight) of capital from one nation to another, via jobs and resources (Chapter 10)
    capitalism
    a way of organizing an economy so that the things that are used to make and transport products (such as land, oil, factories, ships, etc.) are owned by individual people and companies rather than by the government (Chapter 4)
    capitalism
    an economic system in which there is private ownership (as opposed to state ownership) and where there is an impetus to produce profit, and thereby wealth (Chapter 18)
    career inheritance
    a practice where children tend to enter the same or similar occupation as their parents (Chapter 18)
    carrying capacity
    the amount of people that can live in a given area considering the amount of available resources (Chapter 20)
    case study
    in-depth analysis of a single event, situation, or individual (Chapter 2)
    caste system
    a system in which people are born into a social standing that they will retain their entire lives (Chapter 9)
    casual crowds
    people who share close proximity without really interacting (Chapter 21)
    category
    people who share similar characteristics but who are not connected in any way (Chapter 6)
    centenarians
    people 100 years old or older (Chapter 13)
    charismatic authority
    power legitimized on the basis of a leader’s exceptional personal qualities (Chapter 17)
    chattel slavery
    a form of slavery in which one person owns another (Chapter 10)
    class
    a group who shares a common social status based on factors like wealth, income, education, and occupation (Chapter 9)
    class consciousness
    the awareness of one’s rank in society (Chapter 4)
    class system
    social standing based on social factors and individual accomplishments (Chapter 9)
    class traits
    the typical behaviors, customs, and norms that define each class (also called class markers) (Chapter 9)
    clear division of labor
    the fact that each individual in a bureaucracy has a specialized task to perform (Chapter 6)
    climate change
    long-term shifts in temperature and climate due to human activity (Chapter 20)
    closed system
    a system of stratification that accommodates little change in social position. (Chapter 9)
    code of ethics
    a set of guidelines that the American Sociological Association has established to foster ethical research and professionally responsible scholarship in sociology (Chapter 2)
    coercive organizations
    organizations that people do not voluntarily join, such as prison or a mental hospital (Chapter 6)
    cohabitation
    the act of a couple sharing a residence while they are not married (Chapter 14)
    cohort
    a group of people who share a statistical or demographic trait (Chapter 13)
    collective behavior
    a noninstitutionalized activity in which several people voluntarily engage (Chapter 21)
    collective conscience
    the communal beliefs, morals, and attitudes of a society (Chapter 4)
    colorism
    the belief that one type of skin tone is superior or inferior to another within a racial group (Chapter 11)
    commodification
    the changing of something not generally thought of as a commodity into something that can be bought and sold in a marketplace (Chapter 19)
    concentric zone model
    a model of human ecology that views cities as a series of circular rings or zones (Chapter 20)
    conflict theory
    a theory that looks at society as a competition for limited resources (Chapter 1)
    conflict theory
    a theory that examines social and economic factors as the causes of criminal deviance (Chapter 7)
    conformity
    the extent to which an individual complies with group or societal norms (Chapter 6)
    conspicuous consumption
    the act of buying and using products to make a statement about one’s social standing (Chapter 9)
    constitutional monarchies
    national governments that recognize monarchs but require these figures to abide by the laws of a greater constitution (Chapter 17)
    constructivism
    an extension of symbolic interaction theory which proposes that reality is what humans cognitively construct it to be (Chapter 1)
    content analysis
    applying a systematic approach to record and value information gleaned from secondary data as it relates to the study at hand (Chapter 2)
    contested illnesses
    illnesses that are questioned or considered questionable by some medical professionals (Chapter 19)
    continuity theory
    a theory which states that the elderly make specific choices to maintain consistency in internal (personality structure, beliefs) and external structures (relationships), remaining active and involved throughout their elder years (Chapter 13)
    control theory
    a theory that states social control is directly affected by the strength of social bonds and that deviance results from a feeling of disconnection from society (Chapter 7)
    conventional crowds
    people who come together for a regularly scheduled event (Chapter 21)
    convergence theory
    a sociological theory to explain how and why societies move toward similarity over time as their economies develop (Chapter 18)
    core nations
    dominant capitalist countries (Chapter 10)
    cornucopian theory
    a theory that asserts human ingenuity will rise to the challenge of providing adequate resources for a growing population (Chapter 20)
    corporate crime
    crime committed by white-collar workers in a business environment (Chapter 7)
    corrections system
    the system tasked with supervising individuals who have been arrested for, convicted of, or sentenced for criminal offenses (Chapter 7)
    correlation
    when a change in one variable coincides with a change in another variable, but does not necessarily indicate causation (Chapter 2)
    countercultures
    groups that reject and oppose society’s widely accepted cultural patterns (Chapter 3)
    court
    a system that has the authority to make decisions based on law (Chapter 7)
    credentialism
    the emphasis on certificates or degrees to show that a person has a certain skill, has attained a certain level of education, or has met certain job qualifications (Chapter 16)
    crime
    a behavior that violates official law and is punishable through formal sanctions (Chapter 7)
    criminal justice system
    an organization that exists to enforce a legal code (Chapter 7)
    crowd
    a fairly large number of people who share close proximity (Chapter 21)
    crowdsourcing
    the process of obtaining needed services, ideas, or content by soliciting contributions from a large group of people (Chapter 21)
    cults
    religious groups that are small, secretive, and highly controlling of members and have a charismatic leader (Chapter 15)
    cultural capital
    cultural knowledge that serves (metaphorically) as currency to help one navigate a culture (Chapter 16)
    cultural transmission
    the way people come to learn the values, beliefs, and social norms of their culture (Chapter 16)
    cultural universals
    patterns or traits that are globally common to all societies (Chapter 3)
    culture
    a group's shared practices, values, and beliefs (Chapter 1)
    culture
    shared beliefs, values, and practices (Chapter 3)
    culture lag
    the gap of time between the introduction of material culture and nonmaterial culture’s acceptance of it (Chapter 3)
    culture of prejudice
    the theory that prejudice is embedded in our culture (Chapter 11)
    cyberfeminism
    the application to and promotion of feminism online (Chapter 8)

    D¶

    Davis-Moore thesis
    a thesis that argues some social stratification is a social necessity and is functional (Chapter 9)
    debt accumulation
    the buildup of external debt, wherein countries borrow money from other nations to fund their expansion or growth goals (Chapter 10)
    debt bondage
    the act of people pledging themselves as servants in exchange for money for passage, and are subsequently paid too little to regain their freedom (Chapter 10)
    debunking
    looking beyond the obvious to expose falseness by examining merit, logic, and evidence. (Chapter 2)
    degradation ceremony
    the process by which new members of a total institution lose aspects of their old identities and are given new ones (Chapter 5)
    deindustrialization
    the loss of industrial production, usually to peripheral and semi-peripheral nations where the costs are lower (Chapter 10)
    demedicalization
    the social process that normalizes “sick” behavior (Chapter 19)
    democracy
    a form of government that provides all citizens with an equal voice or vote in determining state policy (Chapter 17)
    democratic leader
    a leader who encourages group participation and consensus-building before moving into action (Chapter 6)
    demographic transition theory
    a theory that describes four stages of population growth, following patterns that connect birth and death rates with stages of industrial development (Chapter 20)
    demography
    the study of population (Chapter 20)
    denomination
    a large, mainstream religion that is not sponsored by the state (Chapter 15)
    dependency ratio
    the number of nonproductive citizens (young, disabled, elderly) to productive working citizens (Chapter 13)
    dependency theory
    a theory which states that global inequity is due to the exploitation of peripheral and semi-peripheral nations by core nations (Chapter 10)
    dependent variables
    a variable changed by other variables (Chapter 2)
    depression
    a sustained recession across several economic sectors (Chapter 18)
    design patents
    patents that are granted when someone has invented a new and original design for a manufactured product (Chapter 8)
    deviance
    a violation of contextual, cultural, or social norms (Chapter 7)
    diagnostic framing
    a social problem that is stated in a clear, easily understood manner (Chapter 21)
    dictatorship
    a form of government in which a single person (or a very small group) wields complete and absolute authority over a government or populace after the dictator rises to power, usually through economic or military might (Chapter 17)
    differential association theory
    a theory that states individuals learn deviant behavior from those close to them who provide models of and opportunities for deviance (Chapter 7)
    diffusion
    the spread of material and nonmaterial culture from one culture to another (Chapter 3)
    digital divide
    the uneven access to technology around race, class, and geographic lines (Chapter 8)
    disability
    a reduction in one’s ability to perform everyday tasks; the World Health Organization notes that this is a social limitation (Chapter 19)
    discoveries
    things and ideas found from what already exists (Chapter 3)
    discrimination
    prejudiced action against a group of people (Chapter 11)
    disengagement theory
    a theory which suggests that withdrawing from society and social relationships is a natural part of growing old (Chapter 13)
    doing gender
    the performance of tasks based upon the gender assigned to us by society and, in turn, ourselves (Chapter 12)
    DOMA
    Defense of Marriage Act, a 1996 U.S. law explicitly limiting the definition of “marriage” to a union between one man and one woman and allowing each individual state to recognize or deny same-sex marriages performed in other states (Chapter 12)
    dominant group
    a group of people who have more power in a society than any of the subordinate groups (Chapter 11)
    double standard
    the concept that prohibits premarital sexual intercourse for women but allows it for men (Chapter 12)
    downward mobility
    a lowering of one’s social class (Chapter 9)
    dramaturgical analysis
    a technique sociologists use in which they view society through the metaphor of theatrical performance (Chapter 1)
    dyad
    a two-member group (Chapter 6)
    dynamic equilibrium
    a stable state in which all parts of a healthy society work together properly (Chapter 1)
    dysfunctions
    social patterns that have undesirable consequences for the operation of society (Chapter 1)

    E¶

    e-readiness
    the ability to sort through, interpret, and process digital knowledge (Chapter 8)
    e-waste
    the disposal of broken, obsolete, and worn-out electronics (Chapter 20)
    ecclesia
    a religion that is considered the state religion (Chapter 15)
    economy
    the social institution through which a society’s resources (goods and services) are managed (Chapter 18)
    education
    a social institution through which a society’s children are taught basic academic knowledge, learning skills, and cultural norms (Chapter 16)
    elder abuse
    the act of a caretaker intentionally depriving an older person of care or harming the person in their charge (Chapter 13)
    emergent norm theory
    a perspective that emphasizes the importance of social norms in crowd behavior (Chapter 21)
    empirical evidence
    evidence that comes from direct observations, scientifically gathered data, or experimentation (Chapter 2)
    endogamous marriages
    unions of people within the same social category (Chapter 9)
    environmental racism
    the burdening of economically and socially disadvantaged communities with a disproportionate share of environmental hazards (Chapter 20)
    environmental sociology
    the sociological subfield that addresses the relationship between humans and the environment (Chapter 20)
    epidemiology
    the study of the incidence, distribution, and possible control of diseases (Chapter 19)
    established sects
    sects that last but do not become denominations (Chapter 15)
    ethnicity
    shared culture, which may include heritage, language, religion, and more (Chapter 11)
    ethnocentrism
    the evaluation and judgment of another culture based on one’s own cultural norms (Chapter 3)
    ethnography
    participating and observing thinking and behavior in a social setting (Chapter 2)
    evolutionary model of technological change
    a breakthrough in one form of technology that leads to a number of variations, from which a prototype emerges, followed by a period of slight adjustments to the technology, interrupted by a breakthrough (Chapter 8)
    exchange theory
    a theory which suggests that we experience an increased dependence as we age and must increasingly submit to the will of others, because we have fewer ways of compelling others to submit to us (Chapter 13)
    exogamous unions
    unions of spouses from different social categories (Chapter 9)
    experiment
    the testing of a hypothesis under controlled conditions (Chapter 2)
    explicit rules
    the types of rules in a bureaucracy; rules that are outlined, recorded, and standardized (Chapter 6)
    expressive crowds
    crowds who share opportunities to express emotions (Chapter 21)
    expressive function
    a group function that serves an emotional need (Chapter 6)
    expressive leader
    a leader who is concerned with process and with ensuring everyone’s emotional wellbeing (Chapter 6)
    expulsion
    the act of a dominant group forcing a subordinate group to leave a certain area or even the country (Chapter 11)
    extended family
    a household that includes at least one parent and child as well as other relatives like grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins (Chapter 14)
    extreme poverty
    the state where one is barely able, or unable, to afford basic necessities (Chapter 10)
    exurbs
    communities that arise farther out than the suburbs and are typically populated by residents of high socioeconomic status (Chapter 20)

    F¶

    false consciousness
    a condition in which the beliefs, ideals, or ideology of a person are not in the person’s own best interest (Chapter 4)
    family
    socially recognized groups of individuals who may be joined by blood, marriage, or adoption and who form an emotional connection and an economic unit of society (Chapter 14)
    family life course
    a sociological model of family that sees the progression of events as fluid rather than as occurring in strict stages (Chapter 14)
    family life cycle
    a set of predictable steps and patterns families experience over time (Chapter 14)
    family of orientation
    the family into which one is born (Chapter 14)
    family of procreation
    a family that is formed through marriage (Chapter 14)
    fertility rate
    a measure noting the actual number of children born (Chapter 20)
    feudal societies
    societies that operate on a strict hierarchical system of power based around land ownership and protection (Chapter 4)
    field research
    gathering data from a natural environment without doing a lab experiment or a survey (Chapter 2)
    figuration
    the process of simultaneously analyzing the behavior of an individual and the society that shapes that behavior (Chapter 1)
    filial piety
    deference and respect to one’s parents and ancestors in all things (Chapter 13)
    first world
    a term from the Cold War era that is used to describe industrialized capitalist democracies (Chapter 10)
    flash mob
    a large group of people who gather together in a spontaneous activity that lasts a limited amount of time (Chapter 21)
    folkways
    direct, appropriate behavior in the day-to-day practices and expressions of a culture (Chapter 3)
    formal education
    the learning of academic facts and concepts (Chapter 16)
    formal norms
    established, written rules (Chapter 3)
    formal organizations
    large, impersonal organizations (Chapter 6)
    formal sanctions
    sanctions that are officially recognized and enforced (Chapter 7)
    fourth world
    a term that describes stigmatized minority groups who have no voice or representation on the world stage (Chapter 10)
    fracking
    hydraulic fracturing, a method used to recover gas and oil from shale by drilling down into the earth and directing a high-pressure mixture of water, sand, and proprietary chemicals into the rock (Chapter 20)
    frame alignment process
    using bridging, amplification, extension, and transformation as an ongoing and intentional means of recruiting participants to a movement (Chapter 21)
    function
    the part a recurrent activity plays in the social life as a whole and the contribution it makes to structural continuity (Chapter 1)
    functionalism
    a theoretical approach that sees society as a structure with interrelated parts designed to meet the biological and social needs of individuals that make up that society (Chapter 1)

    G¶

    gatekeeping
    the sorting process by which thousands of possible messages are shaped into a mass media-appropriate form and reduced to a manageable amount (Chapter 8)
    gender
    a term that refers to social or cultural distinctions of behaviors that are considered male or female (Chapter 12)
    gender dysphoria
    a condition listed in the DSM-5 in which people experience a deep sense of distress or discomfort due to incongruence between their sex assigned at birth and their gender identity. This condition replaces "gender identity disorder" (Chapter 12)
    gender identity
    a person’s deeply held internal perception of one's gender (Chapter 12)
    gender role
    society’s concept of how men and women should behave (Chapter 12)
    generalized other
    the common behavioral expectations of general society (Chapter 5)
    generalized others
    the organized and generalized attitude of a social group (Chapter 1)
    genocide
    the deliberate annihilation of a targeted (usually subordinate) group (Chapter 11)
    gentrification
    the entry of upper- and middle-class residents to city areas or communities that have been historically less affluent (Chapter 20)
    geriatrics
    a medical specialty focusing on the elderly (Chapter 13)
    gerontocracy
    a type of social structure wherein the power is held by a society’s oldest members (Chapter 13)
    gerontology
    a field of science that seeks to understand the process of aging and the challenges encountered as seniors grow older (Chapter 13)
    gerotranscendence
    the idea that as people age, they transcend limited views of life they held in earlier times (Chapter 13)
    GINI coefficient
    a measure of income inequality within a country; can be used to compare one country’s inequality to another's (Chapter 10)
    glass ceiling
    an invisible barrier that women encounter when trying to win jobs in the highest level of business (Chapter 12)
    global assembly lines
    a practice where products are assembled over the course of several international transactions (Chapter 18)
    global commodity chains
    internationally integrated economic links that connect workers and corporations for the purpose of manufacture and marketing (Chapter 18)
    global feminization of poverty
    a pattern that occurs when women bear a disproportionate percentage of the burden of poverty (Chapter 10)
    global inequality
    the concentration of resources in core nations and in the hands of a wealthy minority (Chapter 10)
    global stratification
    a comparison of the wealth, status, power, and economic stability of countries as a whole (Chapter 9)
    global stratification
    the unequal distribution of resources between countries (Chapter 10)
    globalization
    the integration of international trade and finance markets (Chapter 3)
    grade inflation
    the idea that the achievement level associated with an A today is notably lower than the achievement level associated with A-level work a few decades ago (Chapter 16)
    grand theories
    an attempt to explain large-scale relationships and answer fundamental questions such as why societies form and why they change (Chapter 1)
    grief
    a psychological, emotional, and social response to the feelings of loss that accompanies death or a similar event (Chapter 13)
    gross national income (GNI)
    the income of a nation calculated based on goods and services produced, plus income earned by citizens and corporations headquartered in that country (Chapter 10)
    group
    any collection of at least two people who interact with some frequency and who share some sense of aligned identity (Chapter 6)

    H¶

    habitualization
    the idea that society is constructed by us and those before us, and it is followed like a habit (Chapter 4)
    hate crimes
    attacks based on a person’s race, religion, or other characteristics (Chapter 7)
    Hawthorne effect
    when study subjects behave in a certain manner due to their awareness of being observed by a researcher (Chapter 2)
    Head Start program
    a federal program that provides academically focused preschool to students of low socioeconomic status (Chapter 16)
    heterosexism
    an ideology and a set of institutional practices that privilege straight people and heterosexuality over other sexual orientations (Chapter 12)
    hidden curriculum
    the informal teaching done in schools that socializes children to societal norms (Chapter 5)
    hidden curriculum
    the type of nonacademic knowledge that people learn through informal learning and cultural transmission (Chapter 16)
    hierarchy of authority
    a clear chain of command found in a bureaucracy (Chapter 6)
    high culture
    the cultural patterns of a society’s elite (Chapter 3)
    homophobia
    an extreme or irrational aversion to gay, lesbian, bisexual, or all LGBTQ people, which often manifests as prejudice and bias (Chapter 12)
    horticultural societies
    societies based around the cultivation of plants (Chapter 4)
    hospice
    healthcare that treats terminally ill people by providing comfort during the dying process (Chapter 13)
    human ecology
    a functional perspective that looks at the relationship between people and their built and natural environment (Chapter 20)
    hunter-gatherer societies
    societies that depend on hunting wild animals and gathering uncultivated plants for survival (Chapter 4)
    hypothesis
    a testable proposition (Chapter 1)
    hypothesis
    a testable educated guess about predicted outcomes between two or more variables (Chapter 2)

    I¶

    ideal culture
    the standards a society would like to embrace and live up to (Chapter 3)
    ideology
    the cultural belief system that justifies a society’s system of stratification (Chapter 9)
    impairment
    the physical limitations a less-able person faces (Chapter 19)
    impersonality
    the removal of personal feelings from a professional situation (Chapter 6)
    in-group
    a group a person belongs to and feels is an integral part of his identity (Chapter 6)
    income
    the money a person earns from work or investments (Chapter 9)
    independent variables
    variables that cause changes in dependent variables (Chapter 2)
    individual mandate
    a government rule that requires everyone to have insurance coverage or they will have to pay a penalty (Chapter 19)
    industrial societies
    societies characterized by a reliance on mechanized labor to create material goods (Chapter 4)
    informal education
    education that involves learning about cultural values, norms, and expected behaviors through participation in a society (Chapter 16)
    informal norms
    casual behaviors that are generally and widely conformed to (Chapter 3)
    informal sanctions
    sanctions that occur in face-to-face interactions (Chapter 7)
    information societies
    societies based on the production of nonmaterial goods and services (Chapter 4)
    innovations
    new objects or ideas introduced to culture for the first time (Chapter 3)
    institutionalization
    the act of implanting a convention or norm into society (Chapter 4)
    instrumental function
    being oriented toward a task or goal (Chapter 6)
    instrumental leader
    a leader who is goal oriented with a primary focus on accomplishing tasks (Chapter 6)
    intergenerational mobility
    a difference in social class between different generations of a family (Chapter 9)
    internally displaced person
    someone who fled his or her home while remaining inside the country’s borders (Chapter 20)
    interpretive framework
    a sociological research approach that seeks in-depth understanding of a topic or subject through observation or interaction; this approach is not based on hypothesis testing (Chapter 2)
    intersection theory
    theory that suggests we cannot separate the effects of race, class, gender, sexual orientation, and other attributes (Chapter 11)
    intersex
    people born with sex characteristics (including genitals, gonads and chromosome patterns) that do not fit typical binary notions of male or female bodies. (Chapter 12)
    interview
    a one-on-one conversation between the researcher and the subject (Chapter 2)
    intimate partner violence (IPV)
    violence that occurs between individuals who maintain a romantic or sexual relationship (Chapter 14)
    intragenerational mobility
    changes in a person's social mobility over the course of their lifetime. (Chapter 9)
    inventions
    a combination of pieces of existing reality into new forms (Chapter 3)
    iron cage
    a situation in which an individual is trapped by social institutions (Chapter 4)
    Iron Rule of Oligarchy
    the theory that an organization is ruled by a few elites rather than through collaboration (Chapter 6)

    K¶

    kinship
    a person’s traceable ancestry (by blood, marriage, and/or adoption) (Chapter 14)
    knowledge gap
    the gap in information that builds as groups grow up without access to technology (Chapter 8)

    L¶

    labeling theory
    the ascribing of a deviant behavior to another person by members of society (Chapter 7)
    laissez-faire leader
    a hands-off leader who allows members of the group to make their own decisions (Chapter 6)
    language
    a symbolic system of communication (Chapter 3)
    latent functions
    the unrecognized or unintended consequences of a social process (Chapter 1)
    leadership function
    the main focus or goal of a leader (Chapter 6)
    leadership style
    the style a leader uses to achieve goals or elicit action from group members (Chapter 6)
    legal codes
    codes that maintain formal social control through laws (Chapter 7)
